# POWER<br>FOR AI

<div style="grid-column:1 / -1;width:100%;min-width:0">
<p style="font-size:max(17px,1rem);line-height:1.58"><strong>A founder’s Fieldbook · 3 November 2026 · Seah Ying Cong</strong></p>
<p style="font-size:max(17px,1rem);line-height:1.58">Imagine choosing where to build useful computing because that is where the people, land and work belong—and knowing dependable electricity can follow. A hospital could expand its computing. A research team could run a larger experiment. A developer could turn a warehouse full of machines into a working service.</p>
<p style="font-size:max(17px,1rem);line-height:1.58">I find the nuclear version of this future especially compelling: manufacture small reactors repeatedly, learn from real hardware, and make dependable energy more abundant. But the first founder decision is where in the chain we can produce a real improvement.</p>

## 01 · The opportunity is local

### A global energy forecast cannot energise a building

The electricity question becomes tangible when a developer has a site, a customer and a date—but no accepted route to power. Buying computing equipment does not create a utility connection. Securing a generator does not secure its fuel. A signed electricity agreement does not necessarily build a new plant nearby.

The demand signal is substantial. The IEA estimated global data-centre electricity consumption at **415 TWh in 2024**, with a **945 TWh Base Case for 2030** in its April 2025 report. These figures cover all data centres, including non-AI uses; the latter is a forecast. Its newer update reports **17% growth in 2025**, alongside tighter equipment supply. [IEA 2025 report](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary) · [IEA April 2026 update](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions)

A terawatt-hour measures energy over time. A megawatt measures the rate of delivery. A site also needs acceptable voltage, outage behaviour and response to changing loads. Annual energy abundance somewhere in a region cannot establish those conditions at a particular substation.

**My inference:** the most useful initial market map follows blocked projects and their actual critical paths. The largest forecast is a poor substitute for knowing which buyer can authorise a purchase that moves a project forward.

## 02 · Four companies hiding in one bottleneck

### Choose the capability the founding team can own

There are several worthwhile businesses here, with very different requirements.

| Entry point | What the customer receives | What the founder must be good at |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment manufacturing | Qualified transformers or switchgear | Factory execution, testing, warranty and working capital |
| Interconnection software | Better intake, studies and milestone handling | Power-system models, operator trust and data integration |
| Onsite power integration | A dependable source at a named site | Fuel, permits, engineering, commissioning and operations |
| Reactor development | A repeatable nuclear energy system | Reactor engineering, fuel, authorisation, construction and long-duration capital |

These are company-boundary choices, rather than a ranking of technologies. A transformer manufacturer can be valuable even if AI demand disappoints. A reactor team can pursue a much larger energy ambition than serving data centres. A software team needs a repeated information problem that someone will pay it to solve.

I would start with independent developers that already control a site and have entered a utility process, but lack a hyperscaler’s procurement organisation. That customer definition is a **hypothesis**, not an observed underserved segment.

The proposed product reconciles engineering requirements, utility milestones and manufacturer-confirmed equipment availability into one decision-grade package. Its first competitor is the developer’s existing engineer and EPC contractor. If they already perform the job well, another platform adds little.

## 03 · The transformer is a physical promise

### Voltage conversion comes with specifications and acceptance

A transformer transfers electrical energy between circuits at different voltages. Switchgear controls and isolates circuits, including during faults. The customer needs equipment compatible with its actual system, backed by testing, transport and an accepted commissioning plan.

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/equipment.svg" alt="Separate dated evidence: DOE distribution transformer lead times in 2019 and 2023; Wood Mackenzie large and generator-step-up transformer provider estimate in a 2025 webinar." style="max-width:600px;background:transparent"><figcaption>Different equipment and vintages. These observations are not November supplier quotes. <a href="https://www.energy.gov/oe/supply-chain-and-market-analysis">DOE</a> · <a href="https://www.woodmac.com/events/power-at-a-price-navigating-equipment-delays-market-shifts-supply-chain-shocks-2025/">Wood Mackenzie public webinar</a></figcaption></figure>

DOE reports distribution-transformer lead times rising from **3–6 months in 2019 to 12–30 months in 2023**, the latest data stated on that page. It also identifies **more than 80,000 varieties** and specification inconsistency as a production issue. Those are distribution figures, not current large-power-transformer or switchgear quotes. [DOE supply-chain analysis](https://www.energy.gov/oe/supply-chain-and-market-analysis)

For large and generator-step-up transformers, Wood Mackenzie’s public **2025 webinar** describes availability as improving slightly while averaging **2–3 years**. The underlying survey methods are not available here. [Wood Mackenzie public description](https://www.woodmac.com/events/power-at-a-price-navigating-equipment-delays-market-shifts-supply-chain-shocks-2025/)

A founder should see a question worth testing: can better reconciliation find an acceptable alternative before a costly commitment? DOE’s recent discussion of refurbishment highlights testing and documentation barriers. Finding nominal stock is only the beginning; a qualified engineer and accepting utility must establish suitability. [DOE, October 2026](https://www.energy.gov/oe/articles/innovative-solutions-grid-equipment-shortages)

## 04 · Onsite power moves the boundary

### The fuel pipe becomes part of the computing system

Onsite generation is attractive because it can reduce dependence on a particular grid construction schedule. A turbine converts fuel heat into mechanical work and electricity. A fuel cell converts fuel electrochemically. Both still need a complete site system, dependable fuel and permission to operate.

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/power-chain-watercolor.png" alt="Watercolor of a power plant, transmission, transformer and open switch leading to waiting servers; an engineer checks the missing connection." style="max-width:600px;background:transparent"><figcaption>The missing link determines readiness. Original generated editorial metaphor, not an engineering layout or operating installation.</figcaption></figure>

The turbine supply chain has its own queue. GE Vernova disclosed **122 GW of gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations** in its October 2026 results. That combined book is neither delivered generating capacity nor a firm order total with identical commitment levels. [GE Vernova Q3 release](https://www.gevernova.com/news/ge-vernova-reports-strong-third-quarter-2026-financial-performance)

Fuel cells offer a useful operating precedent. Bloom and Equinix described approximately **75 MW operational and 30 MW under construction**, supplementing the grid at **19 US data centres**, in February 2025. This is a dated supplier/customer announcement, not an independently metered current fleet assessment or proof of standalone reliability. [Bloom–Equinix announcement](https://www.bloomenergy.com/news/bloom-energy-expands-data-center-power-agreement-with-equinix-surpassing-100mw/)

I would compare qualified partners before inventing new generation hardware. The decision needs firm fuel terms, emissions permissions, net output under site conditions, maintenance coverage and the consequences of an outage. Natural-gas fuel cells remain fuel-dependent; their name does not make their electricity carbon-free.

## 05 · A queue is a request

### Software can improve evidence; it cannot construct transmission

A new generator must be studied because connecting it changes power flows and may require network upgrades. A large new load also needs a credible delivery path. These are related system problems with different applications and datasets.

LBNL’s **2026 Queued Up** edition describes about **8,200 active generation and storage projects** at the end of 2025. For capacity requested during **2000–2020**, it reports **13% operating and 75% withdrawn** by end-2025. This is a historical generation-capacity cohort, not an AI-load queue or the probability that a particular developer will succeed. [LBNL Queued Up](https://emp.lbl.gov/queues/)

PJM describes vetting data-centre construction timelines and financial commitments when preparing its load forecast. A request needs evidence of maturity before it becomes credible demand. [PJM load forecasting](https://learn.pjm.com/three-priorities/planning-for-the-future/load-forecasting)

The software field is already occupied. GridUnity markets large-load workflows from discovery to energisation. Pearl Street offers study automation and scenario analysis. Their public descriptions establish competition, not independently verified speedups. [GridUnity](https://www.gridunity.com/gridinterconnect-for-large-load) · [Pearl Street](https://pearlstreettechnologies.com/)

My proposed wedge therefore starts with a narrow comparison: reconstruct the customer’s actual critical path using its existing tools, then identify an avoidable error the incumbent process missed. If the limiting step is transmission construction, our best deliverable may be an honest no-go decision. That can prevent a bad investment, but it is not proof of a scalable software company.

## 06 · Nuclear deserves a serious hearing

### A controlled chain reaction is an important beginning

A reactor uses a controlled chain reaction to release heat. A heat-removal system carries that energy away. A conversion system turns some of it into electricity, while pumps, controls and other auxiliaries consume part of the output. The customer ultimately needs dependable **net electrical delivery**.

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/nuclear-gates.svg" alt="Conceptual evidence gates from zero-power criticality through sustained heat, net electricity, site permissions and repeat dependable delivery." style="max-width:600px;background:transparent"><figcaption>Conceptual evidence gates, not a universal regulatory sequence. Authorisation and engineering work overlap. A test milestone establishes only what was tested.</figcaption></figure>

The appeal of small reactors is a learning loop: build manageable hardware, test it, discover the difficult interfaces and repeat. **My hypothesis:** if repeated manufacturing and operating experience lower delivered cost and schedule risk, they could make dependable low-carbon energy available to more sites. Smaller size alone does not establish that result.

DOE reports **four advanced test reactors reaching criticality by 4 July 2026** and calls the goal exceeded. But the original overview specified **at least three concepts outside national laboratories**, while the later tally includes tests at Idaho National Laboratory. I retain that unresolved scope mismatch rather than declaring every original condition met. [DOE July fact sheet](https://www.energy.gov/articles/fact-sheet-golden-era-american-nuclear-energy-has-arrived) · [DOE program overview](https://www.energy.gov/ne/us-department-energy-reactor-pilot-program)

The reported test achievements are real progress on a narrower question. They do not establish commercial power delivery. A DOE test pathway and a future commercial site’s permissions must be assessed separately.

## 07 · Valar Atomics and the hardware loop

### The promise is repeatable execution

The name is **Valar Atomics**, rather than “Valor.” DOE confirms that its Ward 250 reactor completed **zero-power fueled criticality on 18 June 2026** at the Utah San Rafael Energy Lab. That establishes a controlled nuclear chain reaction in a test reactor. It does not establish useful electricity supplied to a data centre. [DOE milestone release](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-second-advanced-reactor-achieving-criticality)

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/reactor-watercolor.png" alt="Conceptual watercolor of a reactor heat loop, heat exchanger, separate turbine and generator, and server rack; heat must pass through a conversion system before electricity is useful." style="max-width:600px;background:transparent"><figcaption>Heat is the beginning of the product. Original generated conceptual illustration; not Valar’s actual plant design or a claim of demonstrated electricity.</figcaption></figure>

Valar describes Ward 250 as a high-temperature, helium-cooled reactor using TRISO fuel. Its engineering argument is to learn through extensive hardware testing and iteration. I find that interesting because operational competence has to be built through actual systems. The company’s public update also contains incomplete text and a “July XX” placeholder; I use its design description cautiously and exclude its raw performance/date arithmetic. [Valar engineering update](https://www.valaratomics.com/docs/Ward-250-By-the-Numbers)

The strongest version of the thesis is an integrated capability: design, manufacture, test and operate a repeatable energy system. The decisive next evidence is sustained useful output, then a permitted customer deployment, then a repeat build with measured cost and schedule. These are my diligence gates, not claims about Valar’s present achievements.

A founder with reactor, fuel and operating expertise could rationally choose this path. The public evidence cannot yet rank Valar’s delivered electricity cost against its peers. I would finance a named engineering advance rather than capitalise a promised future cost curve.

## 08 · Compare projects, not company headlines

### Some of the apparent race is between different milestones

| Company | Public evidence at the cutoff | What it leaves unproven |
|---|---|---|
| Valar | DOE-confirmed Ward 250 zero-power criticality | Sustained commercial electrical output ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-second-advanced-reactor-achieving-criticality)) |
| Oklo | Company reports low-power criticality for Groves, an isotope test reactor | Aurora commercial plant operation ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/newsroom/oklos-groves-reactor-achieves-first-criticality-in-under-a-year)) |
| Aalo | DOE confirms Aalo-X zero-power criticality; follow-on power is a separate project | Data-centre electricity from that follow-on ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal), [Aalo](https://www.aalo.com/post/aalo-achieves-first-criticality)) |
| Radiant | October update describes safety-analysis approval and a readiness review before nuclear operations | Criticality or full-power operation from that announcement ([Radiant](https://radiantnuclear.com/news/radiant-secure-doe-approval-for-full-power-operations)) |

The differences matter. An isotope test reactor is not an operating commercial power product from the same company. An approval whose next step is a readiness review is not evidence that the plant has run at full power, even when a headline sounds close.

I would keep a project-level record with separate fields for test authorisation, fueled operation, sustained heat, net electricity, site permissions, customer contract and delivery. A single progress score would hide precisely the uncertainty the buyer needs to understand.

The fair conclusion is encouraging and demanding: hardware milestones are accumulating, while the commercial energy system remains the object to prove.

## 10 · A nuclear deal can mean several things

### Demand support is valuable before electricity exists

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/deal-map.svg" alt="Three distinct nuclear procurement categories: preserve existing generation, restart or uprate a plant, and develop a new reactor. Microsoft’s agreement with Constellation concerns restarting the former TMI Unit 1, now Crane. Constellation’s August 2026 update targets **2027**. The plant was not established as restarted by this research. [Constellation update](https://www.constellationenergy.com/news/2026/08/constellation-reports-second-quarter-2026-results.html)

Google’s Kairos arrangement supports future grid supply. Amazon, X-energy and AEP describe a feasibility-stage Indiana proposal with **12 units and 960 MW**; it remains proposed development. [Kairos](https://www.kairospower.com/google) · [October 2026 joint announcement](https://investors.x-energy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/x-energy-amazon-american-electric-power-partner-bring-smr)

Meta has moved beyond an RFP. Its January 2026 announcement includes existing Vistra plant offtake and future uprates, alongside Oklo and TerraPower development. Its Oklo plan potentially begins as early as **2030**. Existing supply, incremental plant output and proposed new reactors are different categories. [Meta announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/meta-nuclear-energy-projects-power-american-ai-leadership/)

I read these as evidence of serious customer intent and attempts to help finance future supply. I cannot infer from them that every announced megawatt is new, funded to completion, or available to an unrelated developer.

## 11 · Geography changes the answer

### Singapore cannot import an American site plan

Singapore paused new data-centre capacity in **2019** and resumed selective allocations through calls for applications from **2022**, according to the government’s October 2026 retrospective. The same speech distinguishes IT load from whole-facility electricity use and warns against adding overlapping capacity figures. [MDDI speech](https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/closing-speech-by-sms-tan-kiat-how-for-the-second-reading-of-the-digital-infrastructure-bill/)

The Green Data Centre Roadmap targets **at least 300 MW of additional near-term capacity**, with further growth through green energy. This is a policy ambition, not evidence that the capacity is operating or that a particular project can obtain approval. [IMDA roadmap factsheet](https://www.imda.gov.sg/resources/press-releases-factsheets-and-speeches/factsheets/2024/charting-green-growth-for-data-centres-in-sg)

The founder implication is practical. Start with the local allocation, efficiency and acceptable-supply framework. American gas availability or a DOE reactor demonstration confers no Singapore siting permission. I would not sell the US readiness package locally without a fresh engineering and policy assessment.

Geothermal is another serious source of dependable energy. Fervo’s announced Google PPA covers **396 MW expected online in 2028**; that is future contracted supply, not current output. Enhanced geothermal should not be conflated with demonstrated superhot-rock operation. [Fervo announcement](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-and-google-sign-396-mw-ppa/)

I explored that physical frontier in [Clean, Limitless Energy: Superhot Geothermal](/research/superhot-rock-geothermal/). Here the relevant point is portfolio choice: a developer can compare different qualified supply paths while the founder chooses one capability to own.

## 12 · The economics of readiness

### The fee has to survive the engineering

My proposed package contains dated utility evidence, responsible-engineer-reviewed requirements, manufacturer-confirmed quotes and slot expiry, accepted alternatives, commissioning dependencies and a named owner for every blocking decision. It creates no right to certify equipment or guarantee energisation.

## 13 · What cheap power leaves out

### Price the dependable service at the boundary

An onsite comparison needs a consistent denominator: net electricity delivered under the promised conditions. A capital-cost quote omits fuel and operations. A fuel-only calculation omits the plant. A nameplate rating omits auxiliaries and the extra equipment needed when a unit is unavailable.

Our deliberately incomplete gas illustration produces **$82.57, $144.11 and $247.11 per MWh** in low/base/high-cost cases. All fuel, capex, utilisation and financing inputs are invented. The model excludes redundancy, storage, firm pipeline reservation, gas lateral, land, interconnection, standby tariff and other site costs. These are not firm-power LCOEs and must not be compared with a complete utility tariff. [Original gas assumptions and arithmetic](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

In the base illustration, **$1/MMBtu** more fuel adds **$10/MWh**, because the assumed heat rate is **10 MMBtu/MWh**. This sensitivity is arithmetic, not a fuel-price forecast. [Original model](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

Storage answers a different question. An illustrative **20 MW / 80 MWh** battery provides **four rated hours** before losses and reserve. A full day at that load needs **480 MWh**, before allowing for recharge and losses. These are energy/power identities, not a battery design. [DOE storage framework](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/2022-grid-energy-storage-technology-cost-and-performance-assessment) · [Original scenario](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

I have deliberately not estimated a nuclear electricity cost from a criticality announcement. The missing evidence—repeat construction, fuel, operating performance, financing, insurance and site delivery—is the very evidence that determines the answer.

## 14 · The bear case should change the plan

### Scarcity can ease before the startup earns a position

The strongest objection is that the founder mistakes a temporary supply shortage for a durable coordination business. Manufacturers expand. Utilities improve their processes. Customers standardise designs. The capable EPC already knows how to get the project built.

There is real supply-response evidence. Hitachi announced **over $1 billion** in US grid manufacturing investment, including about **$457 million** for a large-power-transformer factory. Eaton announced **over $30 million** for switchgear expansion, targeting production in the **first half of 2027**. These are investment announcements and targets, not already available output. [Hitachi](https://www.hitachi.com/en-us/press/hitachi-announces-historic-1-billion-usd-manufacturing-investment-to-power-americas-energy-future/) · [Eaton](https://www.eaton.com/us/en-us/company/news-insights/news-releases/2026/eaton-expands-operations-in-nebraska-with-new-manufacturing-facility.html)

Demand could disappoint too. The IEA’s **2035 scenarios span 700–1,700 TWh** of global data-centre electricity. Forecast uncertainty is a reason to build a business that earns value on actual projects, rather than lever its balance sheet to every announced campus. [IEA scenarios](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary)

For readiness services, bespoke engineering, long sales cycles and liability could consume the margin. For onsite owners, fuel and performance obligations could overwhelm the apparent spread. For reactor developers, a successful test could still precede a long, expensive path to repeatable commercial operation.

My response is to cap the first commitment at a paid comparison, preserve the option to remain an engineering service, and stop if the evidence workflow does not repeat. A smaller honest business is preferable to assuming software economics around physical work.

## 15 · A path that earns its expansion

### Automate the repeated work after it is visible

<figure><img src="/images/power-for-ai/roadmap.svg" alt="Proposed 12–24 month sequence: validate access and paid pilots, repeat delivery, automate recurring reconciliation, then enter one additional region or onsite evaluation module." style="max-width:600px;background:transparent"><figcaption>Original proposed sequence, not a sales or regulatory forecast. Calendar targets and thresholds are founder choices.</figcaption></figure>

**Months 0–3:** founder and independent electrical engineer reconstruct actual project histories, compare incumbent workflows and sell bounded pilots. Continue only when customers pay and their engineer accepts an actionable improvement. [Proposed validation gates](#card-16-the-first-decision-gate)

**Months 3–6:** repeat the same package with controlled scope. Measure collected fees, actual delivery hours, acquisition effort and rework. Find out whether the second customer buys the same job, rather than a completely new consultancy engagement. [Original proposed sequence](#card-16-the-first-decision-gate)

**Months 6–12:** automate recurring evidence reconciliation: incompatible requirements, expiring quotes, missing acceptance documents and dependencies whose dates lack support. Integrate with existing engineering and utility systems. Keep responsible engineers in control of compatibility decisions. [Original proposed sequence](#card-16-the-first-decision-gate)

**Months 12–24:** expand to one further utility region or a qualified onsite-partner evaluation module after repeat contribution is demonstrated. New tariffs, engineering requirements and authority boundaries need fresh validation. Generation ownership and reactor development would require a separate mandate. [Original proposed sequence](#card-16-the-first-decision-gate)

For a nuclear team, I would use the same capital discipline around a different object: the next measurable plant advance. The gated evidence would concern useful output, operational reliability, fuel and repeat construction rather than readiness-service fees.

## 16 · The first decision gate

### Leave with evidence of a business, or stop

The founder owns the commercial test; an independent qualified electrical engineer owns technical challenge. These are **proposed thresholds**, not market benchmarks. No interviews or pilots have occurred.

**First 30 days:** interview **12 decision makers across at least four developers** and review **six delayed-project histories**, with permission. Continue only if **three histories** show material avoidable errors accepted by the customer’s engineer. Include cancellation cases and the customer’s best existing workflow. [Original validation proposal](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

**Days 31–60:** offer **three bounded pilots** and require **two paid engagements**, engineer acceptance without unresolved critical compatibility errors, and evidence of an actionable critical-path improvement or a costly mistaken commitment avoided. An initial **30-day preventable-delay target** is a screening proposal, never a promise; a revised forecast is not an actual saved day. [Original validation proposal](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

**Days 61–90:** measure collected fee, delivery and acquisition hours, rework and legal/insurance burden; seek a follow-on order. An illustrative **$25,000 contribution per package at 12 packages/year** merely covers the invented **$300,000 overhead** before a buffer. Replace these assumptions with actual commitments before hiring. [Original model](#card-17-the-model-and-the-evidence-boundary)

Stop a broad software build if access is unavailable, incumbents solve the job more cheaply, engineering cannot accept the deliverable, customers will not pay, or little work repeats. Never substitute unsafe equipment to satisfy a delay metric.

My decision is to test a company that makes the route to power more trustworthy. I would keep watching Valar and its peers with equal interest—and ask each new result exactly which part of the journey from nuclear heat to dependable customer electricity it has proved.

## 17 · The model and the evidence boundary

### Read the assumptions before believing the margin

**Evidence cutoff:** 3 November 2026. Public government, laboratory, issuer and original research-provider pages support the factual claims. Company statements establish announced milestones or plans, not independently measured fleet performance. Paid Wood Mackenzie research, private supplier quotes, customer records and site engineering were not available. Last Energy’s announcement body and some NRC pages remained inaccessible; the narrative narrows claims accordingly.

**Original service model, USD; every input invented.** Low/base/high fees: **40,000 / 75,000 / 100,000**. Engineer hours: **200 / 140 / 100**, at **150/hour**. Operations hours: **100 / 60 / 40**, at **100/hour**. Other direct cost: **4,000 / 3,000 / 2,000**. Rework/risk reserve: **5% of fee**. Acquisition cost: **8,000 / 6,000 / 4,000**.
