# THE TIME BETWEEN<br>MOLECULE AND MEDICINE

### A promising treatment should spend less time waiting for its chance to be tested.

**A Fieldbook by Seah Ying Cong · 3 November 2026**

<p style="grid-column:1 / -1">I want a patient to reach a well-designed clinical study sooner because the protocol makes sense, the site is ready, and the people responsible have the same evidence in front of them. There is something profoundly useful to build in the distance between a promising molecule and an answer in humans.</p>

<figure style="grid-column:1 / -1;margin:0"><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/evidence-bridge.png" alt="A researcher organizes tangled documents into a bridge toward a clinician and patient." style="max-width:680px;background:transparent"><figcaption>The bridge is preparation, evidence and coordination. The clinical encounter still has to happen. Original watercolor metaphor; no measured acceleration implied.</figcaption></figure>

<p style="grid-column:1 / -1"><strong>My starting bet:</strong> test a focused trial-startup service for small biotechs before building broad software. Sell a sponsor-accepted feasibility and readiness package. Earn the right to promise an earlier clinical milestone by measuring whether the work actually moves it.</p>

## 01 · More beginnings need better passages

### A candidate is the beginning of the question

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/question-to-study.png" alt="A researcher passes a molecule proposal through a protocol toward a clinician and patient." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>A candidate becomes a clinical question through a protocol and a real encounter. Original watercolor metaphor; no increase in programme supply or clinical success asserted.</figcaption></figure>

In [Pipeline in a Person](/research/pipeline-in-a-person/), I explored the possibility of a small founder-led company coordinating the work needed to create a therapeutic asset. This Fieldbook asks what happens when such a programme reaches people.

A clinical trial turns a biological proposal into a testable question: does this intervention produce a useful outcome, with acceptable risks, in the intended patients? The protocol specifies who can participate, what happens to them, what gets measured, and how the comparison will support an answer.

AI could increase the supply of promising programmes. That is a motivation for this company, **not an established estimate of future trial demand**. Financing, manufacturing and biological failure can stop a programme before a patient ever enrolls. Anthropic’s reported work with BMS already places AI inside study-plan assessment, but supplies no controlled result for shorter trials. [Primary case studies](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development)

I am interested in the founder who makes a valid experiment easier to start and complete. The opportunity is the work between those verbs: preparing, checking, obtaining access, coordinating, and earning acceptance.

## 02 · The clock that matters

### A faster task must move a dependent milestone

Imagine that a protocol packet becomes ready while site contracting and drug supply are still unfinished. The team has saved effort. The first patient may arrive on exactly the same day.

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/critical-path.svg" alt="Parallel readiness branches converge before startup; improving a branch changes the milestone only when it changes the binding dependency."><figcaption>Original dependency diagram. No measured durations. This is the operating logic behind the compression thesis.</figcaption></figure>

The **critical path** is the sequence of dependent work that determines the earliest possible finish. My inference is that an AI service creates trial-time value only when its improvement changes that path. It can also create labor or quality value, but the buyer should price those benefits separately.

I would distinguish receipt of complete inputs, accepted preparation, site activation, first patient first visit, enrollment completion, and database lock. Database lock is the point at which the cleaned study data are finalized for analysis. These are different events, with different owners.

That distinction changes the product. A document generator reports time to output. A trial-startup company must explain what accepted output unlocks, what still blocks progress, and who can resolve it.

## 03 · A protocol meets a real clinic

### Every extra requirement has to fit someone’s life

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/clinic-fit.png" alt="A clinician and patient consider a long protocol beside a clinic doorway and everyday responsibilities." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Protocol requirements meet clinic capacity and the participant’s life. Original watercolor metaphor; amendment burden is not assumed preventable.</figcaption></figure>

An eligibility rule can make the scientific comparison cleaner while excluding people the site actually sees. A visit can improve measurement while making participation difficult for someone with work or caring responsibilities. A test can be scientifically sensible and operationally unavailable at a proposed site.

This is why I would put a clinician, a statistician and a site operator beside the software. The clinician challenges safety and relevance. The statistician challenges whether the design can answer the question. The operator challenges whether the work can actually be delivered.

There is substantial amendment burden, but also a strong warning against assuming it is all preventable. A **sampled observational Tufts benchmark** covering [950 protocols from 16 organizations found 76% were amended; 77% of amendments were judged unavoidable](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38438658/). The cohort included pandemic disruption. Changes in science and regulatory requests can be legitimate reasons to change a study.

Historical cost estimates establish that amendments can be expensive. They do not establish a modern service price or the amount a startup could save. I would price from observed work and customer value rather than a percentage of an old trial-cost headline. [Tufts cost abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30227022/) · [Pivotal-trial cost study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30264133/)

## 04 · Several clocks, one patient journey

### Long elapsed time is evidence to investigate

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/clocks.svg" alt="Separate zero-based bars for historical Phase II initiation, enrollment and total protocol-to-database-lock mean durations."><figcaption>Historical sampled observational means, in days; Tufts duration study, Table 6. Different interval definitions, not additive stages of an average trial. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9373886/">Full paper</a>.</figcaption></figure>

In this pre-pandemic cohort, Phase II mean initiation was [130.0 days, enrollment 438.5 days, and final protocol to database lock 1,027.9 days](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9373886/). The full study covered [187 protocols across 20 participating organizations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9373886/). It is neither a current small-biotech benchmark nor an atopic-dermatitis forecast. **Do not sum these intervals.**

Long duration creates room for inquiry, not an automatic savings pool. Some time is waiting for a signature; some is observing an outcome that cannot yet be known. The same amendment benchmark reports a [260-day mean from identifying the need to the last oversight approval](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38438658/). That interval is not necessarily additional critical-path delay.

I would ask the buyer to reconstruct actual dates and dependencies from recent projects. Without that reconstruction, “faster trials” is too vague to be a product specification.

## 05 · What the new AI evidence earns

### A narrow result can still be valuable

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/ai-boundary.svg" alt="A task-level batch-record review result is separated from downstream clinical-trial milestones." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>The reported task result and the unmeasured downstream outcome occupy different evidence levels. Conceptual reconstruction of the company-reported evidence discussed below; no total-trial acceleration inferred.</figcaption></figure>

Anthropic reports an [89% reduction in human review time at Enveda](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development). That is an exciting company-reported task result. It concerns quality review of executed manufacturing batch records, rather than total clinical-trial duration.

Enveda’s technical evaluation used [four records containing 1,061 pages from one clinical campaign](https://enveda.com/CMC-Document-Processor/). The report says all human-identified actionable issues were matched across runs; that does not mean every run found every issue. It also explicitly describes the system as advisory and **not validated as a GMP computerized system**. Human quality staff retain disposition decisions. [Technical report, evaluation and compliance sections](https://enveda.com/CMC-Document-Processor/)

BMS’s reported application is study-plan assessment. Its public case describes early use and planned expansion, without a controlled trial-duration or success-rate improvement. [Anthropic case study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development)

The product lesson I draw is specific: retrieve evidence, compare versions, flag contradictions, and make expert review easier to audit. Then measure the accepted workflow. The published case does not establish the size of the downstream opportunity.

## 06 · A match is an invitation to investigate

### Records do not walk into a clinic

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/patient-paths.png" style="background:transparent" alt="A medical folder points toward a patient-clinician conversation and an empty chair, with a curved arrow returning for review."><figcaption>A record can lead to assessment, a declined invitation, or another review. Original watercolor metaphor for the gap between a match and a participant; no funnel conversion rates implied.</figcaption></figure>

A recruitment system has to move through accessible records, clinical eligibility, permission to contact, willingness to participate, consent, randomization and retention. A model that improves chart prioritization addresses part of that chain.

The denominator matters. A historical cardiovascular registry study found [11% of 8,900 closed trials were terminated; poor accrual accounted for 41% of terminated trials](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5577897/). That is evidence of consequential recruitment failure in a bounded cohort, not a universal failure rate. The source itself has a start-year inconsistency, recorded in the [evidence notes](#card-20-the-evidence-boundary).

For the proposed wedge, public registry data help identify programmes and competing studies. They do not establish hospital patient access, eligible willing people, or live site capacity. [ClinicalTrials.gov API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/api)

I would expand into matching only with a participating site, lawful access, clinical adjudication and a way to measure what happens after the match.

## 07 · Change where the visit happens

### Flexibility has an operating cost

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/visit-network.png" alt="A clinic coordinates a remote consultation and home visit, each returning measurement records." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Locations can change while evidence and oversight still return to a common accountable workflow. Original watercolor mechanism; no speed benefit implied.</figcaption></figure>

Some trial activities can happen through telehealth, home visits or local healthcare professionals. FDA’s [September 2024 final guidance on decentralized elements](https://www.fda.gov/media/167696/download) describes these options while retaining the same regulatory requirements.

That can reduce a participant’s burden. It also introduces questions about product delivery, local assessments, safety escalation, missing data and consistency of measurement. A home visit that is more convenient but less comparable may change the evidence produced. [FDA design and oversight guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/167696/download)

I would choose decentralized elements procedure by procedure. Ask whether the activity can be conducted reliably outside the central site, who is accountable, how the result reaches the study record, and what happens when it is late or abnormal.

FDA’s [September 2025 final E6(R3) guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e6r3-good-clinical-practice-gcp) supports flexible, risk-based approaches and technology. It preserves responsibilities; transferring work to a provider does not remove ultimate sponsor responsibility. [E6(R3), section 3.6.6](https://www.fda.gov/media/169090/download)

My inference is that convenience can be a product advantage only when the evidence and oversight still work. “Decentralized” describes a design choice; it is not a measured speed benefit by itself.

## 08 · A control is a comparison

### Predicting a patient is different from observing one

A trial needs a credible account of what would have happened without the intervention. Random assignment helps make groups comparable. External controls instead use outcomes observed outside the treated study. Digital twins predict prognosis. Those approaches need different arguments for validity.

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/design.svg" alt="Trial design choices preserve evidence obligations: remote visits alter location, externally observed outcomes and predicted prognosis serve different roles."><figcaption>Conceptual distinctions from FDA guidance, not a claim of interchangeable methods or regulatory acceptance. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download">External-control draft</a> · <a href="https://www.fda.gov/media/167696/download">Decentralized elements</a>.</figcaption></figure>

The FDA external-control guidance remained a [February 2023 draft, not for implementation, when checked](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-design-and-conduct-externally-controlled-trials-drug-and-biological-products). Its scientific warning matters: unmeasured confounding and other bias cannot simply be eliminated. If comparison patients differ in unrecorded ways, a model may confidently explain the wrong difference. [Draft, design considerations](https://www.fda.gov/media/164960/download)

FDA’s real-world-evidence guidance also emphasizes prespecification, access and verification obligations. A data license suitable for analytics may not grant the access needed to support a regulatory submission. [RWE final guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/171667/download)

I would treat external-control work as a specialist, programme-specific business. It is an ambitious opportunity, with a different validation burden from startup preparation.

## 09 · Singapore as a place to learn

### A defined process is useful; patient access still has to be earned

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/approval-path.svg" alt="Authority and ethics reviews run in parallel, while site readiness remains a separate dependency." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Parallel review is a process option, not a promise of a ready site. Original conceptual synthesis of the HSA requirements linked below; no elapsed startup forecast.</figcaption></figure>

Singapore provides a concrete setting in which to understand responsibilities and process. HSA lists a [standard CTA turnaround of 30 working days](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/other-regulations/clinical-trials/application/apply-cta-ctn/). Clarification stop clocks are excluded. A clinical trial authorisation application can proceed in parallel with ethics review; that does not mean the site is ready when either finishes. [HSA application requirements](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/other-regulations/clinical-trials/application/apply-cta-ctn/)

I would not borrow the shorter notification route as a new-drug shortcut: CTN applies to a different eligible category. Nor would I extrapolate Singapore’s process across Southeast Asia. [HSA route definitions](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/other-regulations/clinical-trials/application/apply-cta-ctn/)

HSA also preserves overall sponsor responsibility when work is transferred and describes the local-sponsor requirements. Supplying a tool does not automatically make its vendor the trial sponsor; the contracted role needs to be explicit. [Sponsor guidance](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/other-regulations/clinical-trials/conducting-clinical-trials/sponsor/)

For discovery, I would propose outpatient atopic dermatitis, conditional on a qualified clinical lead and willing sponsor and sites. That is a focus hypothesis, not an evidence-selected best market. The missing evidence is practical: investigator access, competing studies, staffing, permissions and the population a site can actually reach.

## 10 · Sell something that can be accepted

### The first product is a readiness package

My proposed buyer is the small biotech’s clinical-operations budget owner preparing an outpatient Phase II study. The sale is a bounded package with a qualified sponsor acceptance rubric.

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/wedge.svg" alt="Evidence matrix, expert issue log, site-confirmed feasibility and a dependency plan converge on sponsor acceptance; clinical execution remains outside scope."><figcaption>Proposed scope and handoff. This is an offer to test, not a validated service or a promise of approval.</figcaption></figure>

The package contains a source-linked eligibility and procedures matrix, an expert issue log, a feasibility matrix grounded in site-confirmed facts, version-consistency checks, and a dependency plan with named owners. It preserves unresolved questions as well as resolved ones.

Clinical care, drug supply, site grants, full monitoring and regulatory decisions stay outside this first fee. A clinical lead and statistician review scientific content; the sponsor’s qualified reviewers accept the result. The company commits to preparation and agreed corrections.

I would add an accelerated-startup commitment only after delivery evidence supports it. The distinction is commercially useful: it tells a buyer exactly what they can hold the company accountable for today, while leaving room to own more execution later.

## 11 · Service first is a learning strategy

### Software needs repeated work and a route to adoption

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/service-learning.png" alt="A founder and expert sort evidence folders through a reusable template, leaving awkward papers aside." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Service work can reveal a repeated module; the papers that do not fit represent customization that can consume its value. Original watercolor metaphor, not observed repeatability.</figcaption></figure>

A service gives the founder direct contact with the problem: incomplete inputs, expert disagreements, site reality, acceptance and collections. It can reveal which work repeats and what a buyer considers valuable.

It can also become bespoke consulting. Every sponsor may need a different workflow. Qualified reviewers can become the capacity constraint. The founder can finish projects without accumulating a reusable product.

Software changes the risk. It may scale the repeated task, but it requires data rights, integration, validation, support and enterprise purchasing before those costs are understood. A CRO can also bundle a similar feature into an existing relationship. Medidata already describes an integrated trial-lifecycle platform; established CROs already sell execution. [Medidata platform](https://www.medidata.com/en/clinical-trial-products/unified-platform/) · [Medpace filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1668397/000166839726000006/medp-20251231.htm)

I would start with service **unless the founding team already has unusually strong buyer access and a repeated software job**. The switch condition is evidence that unrelated customers can use the same module with bounded configuration and a common validation package. Paid adoption matters more than the amount of code extracted from consulting.

## 12 · The buyer must win too

### Positive supplier contribution is insufficient

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/economics.svg" alt="Invented base scenario separates supplier fee and costs from buyer avoidable cash benefit; supplier contribution is positive while buyer benefit is below the fee."><figcaption>Original USD scenario, not quotes, market rates or observed savings. Supplier contribution includes acquisition, but precedes fixed overhead. <a href="#card-19-the-model-notes">Assumptions and arithmetic</a>.</figcaption></figure>

My invented base fee is [$90,000 per accepted package](#card-19-the-model-notes). Delivery and reserve consume [$56,200; acquisition another $9,000; contribution is $24,800](#card-19-the-model-notes). Those figures are a test of arithmetic, not a finding about willingness to pay.

Now take the buyer’s side. Suppose the workflow saves [30 days, half of which advance the critical path, with $4,000 of documented avoidable cash per day](#card-19-the-model-notes). Benefit is [$60,000, leaving negative $30,000 after the fee](#card-19-the-model-notes), before customer integration costs.

At that assumed daily cash value, the fee needs [22.5 attributable days of advancement](#card-19-the-model-notes) just to cover itself. An earlier scientific answer may have strategic value too, but I would ask the buyer to establish it, rather than invent a drug valuation to rescue the model.

This is the unresolved commercial question. A company can deliver efficiently and still sell something the customer should decline.

## 14 · The customer already has alternatives

### Compete with the whole existing workflow

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/competition.svg" alt="Existing vendors occupy execution, lifecycle software, provider workflows, data networks, prognosis, recruitment and therapeutic asset ownership."><figcaption>Documented business propositions as of 3 November 2026; not a hands-on scorecard, outcome comparison or exhaustive market map. [IQVIA](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1478242/000162828026008322/iqv-20251231.htm), [ICON](https://investor.iconplc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/icon-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-and) and [Medpace](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1668397/000166839726000006/medp-20251231.htm) bring clinical execution and existing purchasing relationships. Medpace already targets small and midsized biopharma. Small sponsors are an occupied segment.

[Medidata](https://www.medidata.com/en/clinical-trial-products/unified-platform/) describes lifecycle software. My defensibility hypothesis is an indication-specific operating system of accepted evidence, trusted site relationships and repeatable delivery. Generic drafting alone is an exposed position. A buyer must have a reason to choose the company after comparing incumbent configuration, partnership and doing nothing.

## 15 · What the headline omits

### The hard assets are trust, access and responsibility

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/hidden-work.png" alt="A small document machine rests on a larger assembly of expert hands, access keys and clinical responsibility." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Drafting rests on access, expert work and accountability. Original watercolor metaphor for the operating obligations described below; component sizes are not cost shares.</figcaption></figure>

The strongest bear case is that data access and clinical judgment dominate, while drafting is readily bundled. The founder inherits integration, expert review, QA, sales and liability exposure. A cash-constrained biotech may prefer a single accountable CRO, even if a specialist has a better demonstration. That is a business-risk inference from the documented competitive surfaces, not measured win/loss evidence. [CRO model](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1668397/000166839726000006/medp-20251231.htm) · [Integrated provider model](https://www.paradigmhealth.ai/solutions/sponsors)

Another headline concerns animal testing. FDA’s [April 2025 NAMs roadmap announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-announces-plan-phase-out-animal-testing-requirement-monoclonal-antibodies-and-other-drugs) addresses context-specific modernization of **preclinical safety**. It does not eliminate the question of efficacy in humans. Nothing in this dossier establishes that the roadmap has already shortened clinical studies.

The commercial omissions are just as consequential: a deposit does not fund all overhead; a cancellation reserve is not comprehensive insurance; a registry record is not a reachable patient; a faster draft is not an accepted submission. Sponsor responsibility persists under [GCP](https://www.fda.gov/media/169090/download).

I would want the actual expert hours, procurement terms, insurer constraints, lawful access rights and buyer-approved cash effects before investing in scale. Those are the expensive questions concealed by a cheap-looking model call.

## 16 · Earn the next stage

### A conditional plan over a clinical workflow

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/roadmap.svg" alt="Proposed evidence gates move from access and paid pilots to quality and milestone testing, repeated delivery, then reusable software with unrelated buyers."><figcaption>Proposed founder plan, not a forecast. All time windows and investment hurdles originate in the research map and are restated below.</figcaption></figure>

**My proposed opening test:** the founder and a qualified clinical-operations lead spend [6 weeks](#card-18-the-company-can-fail-this-test) reconstructing recent timelines with consenting small sponsors. Obtain dated records and current CRO scopes. Interview budget owners and site operators. Locate work the new company can actually control.

**If access and paid interest exist:** use the [next 3–6 months](#card-18-the-company-can-fail-this-test) for paid packages with pre-agreed acceptance criteria and baselines. Track all delivery attempts and customer costs. Keep the scientific design under qualified review.

**During the proposed 6–12 month stage:** repeat within the indication and sponsor type, examine cancellations and collection delays, and test why an incumbent cannot match the result. **During the proposed 12–24 month stage:** test the repeated module with unrelated paying organizations. [Author’s proposed stage gates](#card-18-the-company-can-fail-this-test)

Patient matching and external controls are later branches, each with separate access, quality and validation requirements. They should earn their own investment case rather than enlarge the first sales pitch.

## 17 · Measure the accepted path

### Include the projects that went badly

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/measurement.svg" alt="All attempted packages enter a log separating input waits, work, reviews and external dependencies." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Proposed measurement design: retain failures and cancellations, distinguish workflow effort from the downstream milestone. No pilot results are asserted.</figcaption></figure>

Before delivery, I would agree what complete input means and how the sponsor will accept the output. Record the usual-workflow baseline and all external dependencies before knowing the result.

The project log should separate input waiting, active work, expert review queues, corrections, contracting, authority review and supply. It should record who changed what and why. Report cancelled and failed assignments alongside successful ones. An intention to deliver is a better denominator than a curated set of finished packages.

Quality assessment needs independent qualified reviewers. A plausible output can contain a consequential omission. Count substantive corrections and adjudicated critical safety or eligibility misses, not merely pages generated or comments accepted.

Compare accepted package lead time and downstream final-protocol-to-first-patient timing. A contemporaneous comparable usual workflow is preferable where practical; patient mix, protocol changes and site differences can still confound a tiny operational pilot. Keep causal claims narrower than the evidence.

This is my proposed measurement design. The published AI and duration studies do not validate it, and a small pilot cannot certify safety or estimate a general effect on clinical success. The purpose is to decide whether there is enough evidence to fund a stronger test.

## 18 · The company can fail this test

### Stop rules are part of the ambition

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/decision.svg" alt="Sequential decision gates require lawful access and a paying buyer, quality-preserving critical-path movement, then customer value and company contribution; failed gates trigger stopping or repositioning."><figcaption>Original falsification framework. These are founder-selected investment hurdles, not medical standards or established industry benchmarks.</figcaption></figure>

**Proposed access gate:** within the opening **6 weeks**, inspect recent projects from **3 consenting sponsors**, interview **10 budget owners and 5 site operators**, and proceed only if **2 sponsors** identify a recurring controllable bottleneck, share lawful evidence and agree in principle to a paid bounded pilot. These counts are my test design.

**Proposed delivery gate:** sell **3 packages over 3–6 months**. Seek **zero adjudicated critical omissions**, at least **20% shorter package lead time** without more substantive corrections, and at least **14 calendar days of attributable startup advancement in 2 pilots**. Seek at least **25% contribution after delivery, rework and acquisition**, and paid renewal or referral from **2 sponsors**. These thresholds are assumptions, not results.

A critical omission pauses live use for repair and revalidation. Faster preparation without an earlier milestone falsifies the time-compression claim. Customer value below the price falsifies the commercial offer at that price.

**Proposed expansion gate:** repeat delivery during **months 6–12**; during **months 12–24**, require **2 unrelated paying organizations** using the same software module without a core-code fork. If access requires building a hospital network first, or customization absorbs the value, stay narrow, reposition or stop. This entire card is an original proposed experiment; none of its counts predicts success.

## 19 · The model notes

### Assumptions make the uncertainty inspectable

<figure><img src="/images/clinical-trial-compression/model-equations.svg" alt="Separate supplier contribution and buyer benefit equations show how the assumptions enter the model." style="background:transparent"><figcaption>Original scenario equations. USD inputs and all low/base/high arithmetic are stated below; no market prices or observed savings.</figcaption></figure>

**Original author scenarios, 3 November 2026. All commercial inputs below are invented USD assumptions.** They are not quotes, observed productivity or forecasts.
