# Science evidence notes: pair-bond medicine

Research/access date: 2026-11-03. Internal research slice, not medical advice or a final claim ledger.
Read Fieldbook SKILL.md and all reference files before research. No git commands used.

## Research question and strongest bear case

**Inference:** A transient increase in affiliative behavior is several causal steps from durable relationship health. A therapeutic window is plausible; a programmable attachment outcome is unproved. The strongest bear case is not that bonding biology is imaginary: it is that a context-sensitive, distributed system will resist a reliable, scalable, indication-specific drug product, while skilled non-drug therapy already improves some target outcomes.

**Hypothesis requiring a trial:** A drug adjunct improves durable, independently assessed relationship functioning beyond the same psychotherapy, with acceptable harms and freely maintained consent from both partners. Neither laboratory conflict behavior nor uncontrolled PTSD improvement proves this.

**Disproof gate:** A sufficiently powered, preregistered active-comparator trial finds no clinically meaningful incremental relationship benefit, or gains disappear after acute effects, or coercion/deterioration/adverse events offset benefits. Endpoints must allow healthy separation rather than reward relationship persistence alone.

## S1 — Ditzen et al., acute couple-conflict experiment (2009)

- Publication: Biological Psychiatry 65:728–731, 2009; DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2008.10.011
- Primary full paper read: https://www.psychologie.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:00000000-1563-1d5c-0000-00004d338ca0/BiolPsychiatry-CoupleConflict09.pdf
- **Fact:** 47 heterosexual couples; double-blind randomized oxytocin/placebo. 23 couples received oxytocin, 24 placebo; 40 IU intranasal; 10-minute laboratory conflict discussion. Positive relative to negative coded behavior increased and post-conflict salivary cortisol fell.
- **Limit:** Acute laboratory proxies, not longitudinal satisfaction, divorce prevention, attachment security, or clinical couples therapy efficacy. Small healthy sample; women studied in luteal phase, with hormonal contraceptives, pregnancy and breastfeeding excluded. Written consent and ethics approval reported.
- **Inference:** Useful human mechanistic signal, insufficient product efficacy evidence. Do not headline “oxytocin saves marriages.”
- Reading evidence: web PDF extraction returned four pages/360 lines; methods and figure legend identify allocation and behavioral denominator. Follow-up fetch timed out; initial full-paper extraction succeeded.

## S2 — Walum, Waldman & Young: reliability critique (2016; online 2015)

- Primary full text read using direct HTTP after web PMC challenge: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4690817/
- DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.06.016 ; Biological Psychiatry 79:251–257.
- **Fact:** Reanalysis of prior meta-analyses estimated healthy-sample mean effect d=.28, median sample 49 and power 16%; clinical median sample 26, d=.32 and power 12%. These are model-based estimates conditional on included literature and assumed effects, not a census proving 84–88% of published claims false.
- **Fact:** Authors emphasize low power, publication/reporting bias, low prior odds, effect inflation, preregistration, transparency and larger direct replications. They remain optimistic about genuine oxytocin modulation and improved delivery.
- **Inference:** Do not count many small positive oxytocin papers as independent validation of a platform. Require replicated indication-specific results and credible pharmacology.
- Reading evidence: direct HTTP/BeautifulSoup output included abstract, power calculations, conclusions and recommendations; web open repeatedly produced recaptcha.

## S3 — Leng & Ludwig: delivery and assay critique (2016)

- Primary accepted manuscript read: https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/19764306/accepted_manuscript_Leng_and_Ludwig.pdf
- DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2015.05.003 ; Biological Psychiatry 79:243–250.
- **Fact:** Review argues very little applied peptide reaches CSF, substantial peripheral exposure complicates interpretation, CSF concentration is not proof of target-site receptor engagement, and problematic assays weaken circulating-oxytocin claims. Calls for dose-response studies, controls for peripheral actions, preregistration and open data.
- **Limit:** A 2016 critique is not evidence that every modern delivery method fails. Do not turn uncertainty about route or measurement into “oxytocin never affects the brain.”
- **Inference:** Better delivery and validated target engagement could be a legitimate enabling wedge; neither can be assumed from spray administration or peripheral biomarker changes.
- Reading evidence: 18-page PDF/677 lines; abstract and blood-brain barrier/nose-to-brain sections opened. Alternative research.ed.ac.uk mirror returned 403; pure.ed.ac.uk worked.

## S4 — Sikich et al., SOARS-B autism RCT (2021)

- Published 2021-10-14, NEJM 385:1462–1473; https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2103583
- Primary indexed abstract read: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34644471/
- **Fact:** 24-week randomized placebo-controlled phase 2 study, ages 3–17, 290 enrolled (146 oxytocin/144 placebo), target 48 IU/day. Modified intention-to-treat analyses: 139/138. ABC modified Social Withdrawal change −3.7 versus −3.5; difference −0.2, 95% CI −1.5 to 1.0, P=.61. Secondary outcomes generally did not differ. Adverse-event incidence/severity similar.
- **Limit:** Pediatric autism is not adult relationship distress. This is important failure of translation from experimental/small-trial promise, not a direct null trial of couples treatment or all oxytocin formulations.
- Reading evidence: PubMed lines 238–245 provide methods/results; NEJM full-text 403. Full paper details not independently audited in this slice.

## S5 — Berendzen et al., oxytocin-receptor knockout voles (2023)

- Neuron 111:787–796.e4; online 2023-01-27, issue 2023-03-15. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2022.12.011
- Primary full text read via direct HTTP: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10150797/
- **Fact:** Three independently generated CRISPR null lines retained partner preference and substantial parenting. Figure 3 partner-preference cohorts: line 4 males WT25/mutant21, females WT19/mutant21; line 5 males WT27/mutant27, females WT19/mutant21; line 1 each sex WT8/mutant8. These are assay cohorts, not one trial denominator.
- **Limit:** Constitutive deletion permits developmental compensation; authors explicitly cannot exclude subtle compensatory pathways. Genetic dispensability does not show acute receptor modulation is useless, nor that oxytocin itself has no role; vasopressin receptor redundancy remains possible. Animal attachment assays are not human relationship outcomes.
- **Inference:** Reject single-master-switch rhetoric. Preserve uncertainty rather than saying this “disproves the love hormone.”
- Reading evidence: direct HTTP output included Summary, Figure 3 sample sizes and discussion on compensation; web PMC/PubMed intermittently challenged or returned empty content.

## S6 — Monson/Wagner et al., MDMA-facilitated CBCT (2020)

- Published 2020-12-07; https://doi.org/10.1080/20008198.2020.1840123
- Primary full text read: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7748057/ ; working author PDF: https://www.coupletherapyforptsd.com/wp-content/uploads/Monson-et-al-2020.pdf
- **Fact:** Uncontrolled six-couple study; one partner had PTSD; all couples heterosexual and White. Condensed 15-session CBCT over seven weeks plus two MDMA sessions for both partners. All completed; no serious adverse events; common reactions included diminished appetite, anxiety, headache and jaw tightness. Clinician PTSD pre/post d=2.10; six-month d=2.25. Patient/partner Couples Satisfaction Index pre/post d=.82/.64; six-month 1.22/.80.
- **Limit:** These are within-group effects. No placebo or CBCT-only control, tiny selected sample, expectancy, intensive care and psychotherapy confounding; no reliable estimate of rare harm. Does not establish benefit for non-PTSD couples, attachment enhancement or durable marriage preservation.
- Reading evidence: web 443-line PMC/8-page PDF; direct HTTP retrieved participants, results/Table 3, discussion. Sponsor-affiliated coauthors disclosed in affiliations.

## S7 — Newer pilot, avoid stale “only six couples studied” claim

- Morland et al., online 2026-09-04, Journal of Traumatic Stress; https://doi.org/10.1002/jts.70126 ; PMID 42693902.
- Author abstract retrieved through Europe PMC primary-publication record API: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:42693902&format=json&resultType=core
- **Fact, abstract only:** Single-site open-label eight veteran-partner dyads (16 people), eight-session brief CBCT with two MDMA sessions and dyadic integration incorporating integrative behavioral couples therapy. PTSD pre/post d=1.25; relationship functioning veteran/partner d=1.83/1.29, with moderate rebound at follow-up. No serious adverse events reported.
- **Limit:** Full paper not accessed. No randomized comparator, so no isolated incremental drug effect. PubMed browser/cookie gate blocked abstract; Europe PMC returned full author abstract and bibliographic metadata successfully.

## S8 — Active comparator trial: actual current registry state

- Primary record https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06044675 ; inspected JSON https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06044675
- **Fact as accessed 2026-11-03:** Phase 2, recruiting; randomized, masking NONE, crossover; estimated enrollment 60 individuals; MDMA-assisted CBCT versus CBCT alone, seven weeks. CBCT-only participants may cross over after follow-up. Actual start 2024-11-15; estimated completion 2027-03-31. Last update posted 2026-08-19. `hasResults:false`.
- **Fact:** Excludes inability to give adequate informed consent; major medical/psychiatric risks and unsafe cardiovascular conditions. Tracks adverse events and longer-term benefits/harms. Relationship measures are secondary; feasibility, safety, appropriateness and CAPS-5 are among primary outcomes.
- **Limit:** Registration proves a test exists, not efficacy. Estimated enrollment must not be called completed participants or 60 couples. Open label leaves expectancy confounding even with active psychotherapy comparator.

## S9 — Non-drug comparator: Monson et al. randomized CBCT trial (2012)

- JAMA 308:700–709, 2012-08-15; https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2012.9307
- Primary full text read by direct HTTP: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4404628/
- **Fact:** Forty couples randomized to 15-session CBCT or waitlist (20 each). Between-group mean change difference in clinician PTSD severity −23.21 (95% CI −37.87 to −8.55); patient relationship satisfaction +9.43 (95% CI .04–18.83). Effects maintained at three months.
- **Limit:** Waitlist, not attention-matched therapy; small sample. Patient relationship benefit borderline P=.049; do not imply equivalent demonstrated partner satisfaction benefit. Severe physical/sexual intimate partner aggression in prior year excluded.
- **Inference:** Drug-adjunct developers must improve on an effective therapeutic component, not compare with doing nothing. No head-to-head incremental drug inference follows from contrasting effect sizes across separate trials.
- Reading evidence: direct HTTP read results, methods and severe-aggression exclusions; JAMA page 403 and PMC web recaptcha, direct PMC worked.

## Consent, harms and endpoint design: explicitly normative/inferential

- **Observed research practice:** Studies screen participants and use informed consent, contraindication exclusions and supervised treatment. Six/eight dyads without serious adverse events cannot establish broad safety.
- **Inference/design requirement:** Separate individual screening for coercion, violence and independent wishes; sober prior consent and sober reassessment; withdrawal without partner veto; independent reporting channel; clinician boundaries; monitoring for relationship deterioration and unwanted attachment. These are proposed safeguards, not trial-proven solutions.
- **Inference:** “Closer” is not synonymous with “healthier.” A relationship medicine endpoint must not count remaining in an abusive relationship as success. Binding romantic or sexual commitments made during intoxication cannot stand in for enduring autonomous preference.
- **Unknown:** Incremental benefit, responder selection, adequate blinding/control, long-term relational adverse events, scalable therapist fidelity, and whether either partner can freely refuse in real clinical practice.
