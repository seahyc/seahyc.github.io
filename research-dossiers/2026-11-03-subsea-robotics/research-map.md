# Research map — subsea robotics

Research cutoff: 3 November 2026. Phase 1 only. Decision memo, not a validated business plan.

## Founder decision

Should a Singapore-based founder build an autonomous subsea inspection operator for cable owners, beginning with repeat condition/change surveys of a small number of permissioned, shallow-water telecom cable corridors, delivered through an established marine survey contractor?

**Provisional decision: fund a bounded customer/field validation, not a new vehicle platform.** Initial offer: an accepted, georeferenced comparison against a prior survey, with source sonar/imagery, coverage and uncertainty explicitly reported. Find new exposure, seabed disturbance and visible free spans within a contracted envelope. Use bought or partner-operated vehicles first. A buried cable's internal condition, exact burial depth without an appropriate sensor, live attack prevention and attribution are outside the offer.

This is a hypothesis, not an established underserved niche. BeeX already offers autonomous capture through reporting (E12); EIVA/Kongsberg already demonstrated autonomous pipeline survey (E10). The first experiment must compare procurement of these alternatives with building anything new. If the customer can buy the outcome acceptably today, the founder should partner, choose a demonstrably different bottleneck, or stop.

## Desired reader conclusion

Underwater infrastructure creates serious physical work and real buying categories. Poor communications make local autonomy valuable, but neither incidents nor autonomous demonstrations establish a startup market. The plausible entry is a narrowly accepted inspection outcome with repeat work and recoverable equipment. Earn proprietary autonomy by proving it removes support-vessel time or re-survey cost on the same quality standard. A general Singapore AUV company and a persistent anti-sabotage fleet do not yet clear the investment gate.

## Actors, incentives and boundary

- Economic customer: cable owner/consortium's marine maintenance or asset-integrity budget holder; budget, purchasing entity and survey cadence unverified.
- First commercial channel: survey contractor with vessel access, operating competence and existing customer acceptance. Partnership can reduce entry cost but gives the contractor pricing power and potentially the customer/data relationship.
- Accepting party: owner's competent survey/asset engineer. Security agency is a separate buyer with a separate mission; regulator permission is not commercial acceptance.
- Equipment/sensor OEM: supplies validated navigation, capture and recovery stack. Founder initially owns mission QA, repeat comparison and service delivery obligations, with contractually licensed data rights.
- Geography: Singapore as proposed founding/learning base; only approved Singapore-water jobs initially. Taiwan/other Asian expansion requires a new local operating and permission assessment. No assumption of transferable naval eligibility or Singapore membership of an allied procurement bloc.
- Horizon: conditional 12–24 month attack plan; early gates below occur before fleet purchase.

## Core assumptions to validate

A1. Owners have repeat surveys whose actionable changes justify incremental spend, rather than relying mainly on fault detection, burial, route diversity, surface monitoring and repair readiness.
A2. Enough eligible segments are inspectable with the selected sensor package; buried cable, turbid water, currents and traffic do not eliminate the narrow beachhead.
A3. Contractor and owner accept comparable evidence from a new supplier and license baseline/raw data for comparison.
A4. Full job savings survive charter, mobilisation, weather, aborted missions, recovery, review and insurance costs.
A5. A founder can retain repeat revenue and reusable product learning rather than supply bespoke engineering at contractor margins.
A6. Components, software and engineering transfers are lawful for the actual origin, configuration, destination and end user. Civil intent alone does not answer classification.

## Strongest bear case

The founder creates an ordinary marine survey subcontractor with an expensive robotics R&D department. Owners may prioritise existing fault response, burial, diverse routes, vessel monitoring and rapid repair over another survey. This is a substitution hypothesis, not a measured buyer preference. Important cable sections are buried or inaccessible to the chosen sensors. The owner buys infrequent campaigns through trusted incumbents; there is no sufficient repeat workload. BeeX is a local counterexample to the claim that nobody serves this space. Kongsberg, Saab, Exail, Oceaneering and established survey companies already integrate the hard technology and procurement relationships.

At sea, a cheap robot still needs a launch and recovery plan, skilled operators, liability cover and sometimes an expensive vessel. Weather, permissions and revisits consume days. A lost vehicle can erase a year of contribution. Defence adds testing, integration, sustainment, eligibility and contract timing; Ghost Shark's multi-year supported contract is evidence of budget, not a fast startup sales cycle. GAO records approximately five years of Orca prototype delays. OEM component supply is attractive only after reliability and design wins, not because an inventor can machine a housing. Export restrictions can affect parts and remote technical support as well as finished vehicles. The scenario base remains a loss-making company even with positive job contribution.

## Falsifiers and explicit stop/go gates

These thresholds are founder-designed screening rules, not industry standards or certification.

1. **Access/demand, day 45:** founder interviews at least eight relevant owner/contractor purchasing or technical leads, obtains three permissioned historical job packs and two independently priced incumbent alternatives. Go only with one owner and contractor agreeing a paid scoped comparison plus access/data rights. Stop if only unpaid demonstrations or defence interest without a funded route appear.
2. **Envelope, day 60:** survey lead and owner agree measurable coverage, positional uncertainty, sensor limits, critical change classes, abort/recovery criteria, permissions and insurance. Stop if useful eligible segments cannot be observed safely and lawfully with an affordable existing stack. No invented universal metre-resolution requirement.
3. **Economic screening, days 60–120:** at least six matched campaigns across at least two distinct condition bands, subject to access. Include every attempt, weather day and revisit. Proposed hurdle: >=20% lower full cost per accepted kilometre at equivalent contracted coverage/quality, versus the best existing alternative; independent engineer adjudicates changes blindly where practical. Require no observed critical misses relative to reference, reporting uncertainty and abstention. Small samples do not prove rare-event safety or field reliability.
4. **Commercial repeat, month 6:** two paid repeat scopes (not letters of interest), collections under agreed terms, permissioned data rights, and quote-backed break-even at a feasible annual demand/capacity level. Stop new hardware spend if credible repeat volume cannot support full company fixed costs and replacement.
5. **Autonomy investment, months 6–12:** fund a specific mission capability only when logs identify a repeated removable cost; require safe recovery after link loss. Do not label remote engineers or re-survey work as eliminated labour.
6. **Scale, months 12–24:** replicate with a second owner/site type only after actual contribution, renewal, recovery and cash timing hold. Local permits and export classification repeated for new geography/configuration.
