# Clinical trial compression — research map

Phase 1 only · 3 November 2026 · author research, not a validated business plan.

## Founder decision

Should a founder build a technology-enabled trial-startup service for small biotechs preparing an outpatient Phase II study, beginning with one indication and selling a sponsor-accepted protocol-feasibility and startup-readiness package; or sell software into CRO/sponsor workflows?

**Provisional decision:** test the bounded service before building a broad platform. Initially choose atopic dermatitis as a discovery cohort, conditional on access to a qualified clinical lead, sponsor and sites. It is a proposed focus, not an evidence-proven best indication. The service owns preparation, traceability, review coordination and a contractual handoff; sponsor/investigator retain decisions and responsibilities. Do not initially become a full CRO or guarantee enrollment, regulatory approval or trial success.

**Desired reader conclusion:** a trial is an evidence-producing system whose critical path contains both reducible coordination and scientifically necessary observation. A founder can test whether better preparation changes an actual milestone. Neither a fast document nor a large candidate pipeline establishes that a trial will finish earlier. Service-first is an experiment with an explicit switch-to-software condition, not an inevitable end state.

## The possibility and mechanism

Human possibility: fewer patients wait while a promising treatment is stalled by missing records, unrealistic criteria or an unusable site plan. This is an ambition, not a claim that a candidate works.

Mechanism: draft protocol → clinician/statistician challenge of endpoints and eligibility → consented/authorized site feasibility, including competing studies and staff availability → version-consistent protocol/site packet → independent sponsor acceptance → site activation → screened → eligible → consented → randomized → retained → endpoint observed → locked data → report. AI can retrieve source evidence, compare versions, expose contradictions and draft; competent people resolve uncertainty. Faster matching cannot manufacture willing participants or valid outcomes.

**Clock definitions:** workflow lead time = receipt of agreed complete inputs to sponsor acceptance of the package. Startup lead time = final protocol to first patient first visit (FPFV), with clinical and operational responsibilities defined. Clinical readout = protocol-defined follow-up and data readiness, not a writing timestamp. Record input-wait, reviewer queue, authority review, contracting and drug-supply dependencies separately; do not subtract elapsed intervals that overlap. A workflow improvement counts as trial compression only to the extent it moves the dated critical-path milestone. Report intent-to-deliver on all assigned projects, including failures and cancellations.

## Assumptions needing validation

- Small biotech clinical operations leaders have budget and authority to buy a separate preparation service before CRO/site lock-in.
- Material delay is controllable before activation, and early access to draft protocols and site evidence is possible under appropriate agreements.
- A reusable indication-specific workflow can reduce work without losing essential scientific content or adding hidden expert-review cost.
- An expert-led service can charge enough to cover integration, review, sales, liability exposure and rework while providing customer value.
- AI may increase candidate supply; no current causal estimate of AI-driven human-trial demand is established here. Financing, CMC and biological failures can prevent any candidate from entering trials (E01–E03).

## First sellable scope (proposed, not a validated offer)

Deliver one version-controlled package: source-linked eligibility/procedure matrix; clinician/statistician issue log with resolved and unresolved items; site feasibility matrix separating registry leads from site-confirmed staffing, patient access and competing-study assumptions; protocol/site-document consistency checks; and a sponsor-accepted dependency plan with named owners. Acceptance requires the sponsor's qualified clinical/operations reviewers, not merely generated files. The fee covers preparation and agreed corrections. Clinical care, full trial monitoring, site grants, drug supply and regulatory decisions remain outside the package. Initially commit only to controllable preparation scope; add an accelerated-startup milestone commitment only after observed delivery evidence supports it.

## Strongest bear case

The scarce asset is trusted site/data access and clinical judgment, not document generation. CROs and established networks already own these relationships. Small biotech customers are cash-constrained, can cancel, and may prefer a single accountable CRO. Much amendment work reflects changed science or regulator requests; much elapsed time is endpoint follow-up. Sponsor QA review and liability can absorb AI savings. A service can become bespoke consulting with weak margins; software may need long integrations and procurement while incumbent platforms bundle the feature. The scenario's base customer receives less modeled avoidable cash than the fee. A profitable supplier at that price is not proof of a valuable product.

## What the headline omits

- Enveda's 89% is review time on manufacturing records, with human disposition retained; the technical report explicitly says CDP is not validated as a GMP computerized system. Its aggregate issue matching is not perfect detection by every individual run.
- BMS is an early planning case; the checked source reports no controlled trial-duration or success-rate improvement.
- The April 2025 animal-testing roadmap concerns preclinical safety and context-specific NAM adoption, not elimination of human efficacy trials.
- TrialGPT is a research lead; its numerical pilot result is excluded from Phase 2 because fresh full-text confirmation was not undertaken.
- External control patients have observed data; a predicted digital twin is a modeled prognosis. Prognostic adjustment in a randomized trial and replacing a control arm require different validity arguments. No blanket FDA approval is demonstrated.
- Old cost cohorts are not current prices. CRO revenue is not software TAM; backlog is not guaranteed cash. Registrations are not eligible, willing or contactable patients.

## Falsifiers and next experiment

All following counts, windows and thresholds are proposed test design, not measured results or industry benchmarks.

**Owner:** founder + named qualified clinical-operations lead (to recruit). **Next 6 weeks:** inspect recent timelines from 3 consenting small biotech sponsors; interview 10 budget owners and 5 investigator/site operators without promising trial outcomes. Require actual dated records and current CRO SOWs, not pain anecdotes. Go only if at least 2 sponsors identify a recurring pre-activation bottleneck with controllable scope, share sufficient evidence lawfully, and agree in principle to a paid, bounded pilot. Stop if access requires building a multi-hospital data network first or the alleged bottleneck is predominantly uncontrollable.

**Next 3–6 months, if gate passes:** sell 3 paid packages with pre-agreed input completeness, acceptance rubric and baseline. Measure all expert and integration hours, substantive corrections, excluded records, critical omissions, elapsed input-to-acceptance, downstream final-protocol-to-FPFV, acquisition cost, collections and customer avoidable spend. Use blinded independent clinical review and comparable usual workflow where practical; a tiny pilot cannot estimate a general efficacy effect.

**Go:** zero adjudicated critical safety/eligibility omissions; at least 20% reduction in package lead time without more substantive corrections; evidence of at least 14 calendar days of attributable startup advancement in at least 2 pilots, including all dependencies; contribution after delivery/rework/acquisition at least 25%; and paid renewal/referral from 2 sponsors. These are investment hurdles. Any critical omission pauses live use; repair and revalidate. Stop or reposition if faster preparation does not advance startup or customers cannot document value exceeding price. Do not claim enrollment or readout compression until observed.

**6–12 months:** repeat within same indication and sponsor type; identify why incumbent workflow cannot match results; test role-specific capacity, DSO and downside cancellations. **12–24 months:** expand only after repeat positive contribution and quality; test software with 2 unrelated paying organizations using the same module and validation package, no core-code fork. If custom integration exceeds delivered value, remain a focused service or stop. External controls require separate program-specific statistical and regulatory gates.
