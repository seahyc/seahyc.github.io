## R1 — October policy is draft and nonbinding

Published 30 October 2026, FR Doc 2026-22192, pages 68526–68527; docket HHS-OASH-2026-0496. High confidence as to the published notice.

The notice calls the policy a **draft**, provides two weeks after publication for comments, and says it creates no rights and binds neither HHS nor the public. It describes intended enforcement discretion while the 503A bulks list proceeds through rulemaking and reserves reconsideration for safety or quality signals. Compounded drugs remain unapproved. Do not describe this as final rulemaking or FDA approval.

Exact verification locations: rendered lines 25–40 (scope/date/comments); 50–82 (draft, nonapproval, intended discretion, reversibility, nonbinding status); 90–95 (current-policy document link).

Decision impact / inference: a verification service may have an emerging compliance job, but an entrant must price regulatory reversibility and get counsel to resolve effective-date and state-law treatment. This notice alone does not establish a settled national launch route or demand.

## R2 — HHS policy body: evidence job and route restrictions

Direct source: https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-503a-peptide-enforcement-discretion-policy-appendix-b.pdf
Sixteen-page policy linked by R1; accessed 3 November 2026. The PDF self-describes as “interim enforcement policy”. R1 calls it draft and nonbinding and refers to Appendix A, while the linked filename ends appendix-b.pdf. Preserve this discrepancy; no finalization is inferred.

It covers BPC-157, epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, semax and TB-500. Conditions include patient-specific prescriptions, single-API products, registered original manufacturers meeting CGMP, valid characterization/COAs, reseller manufacturer/COA validation, reporting and labeling. Appendix B limits chemical forms and routes: BPC-157 acetate (subcutaneous/intramuscular/oral/topical), TB-500/epitalon/MOTS-c acetates (subcutaneous/intramuscular), semax free base (intranasal), KPV acetate (oral/topical). Oral bioavailability documentation is expected; epitalon courses are intermittent.

Appendix B reports PCAC support despite contrary CDER recommendations. It lists a much broader characterization panel than purity alone: orthogonal identity, quantitative-reference HPLC assay, net content, impurities, residual solvents, elemental impurities, counterions, water, microbiology and endotoxin, plus applicable structure/activity work. This is a draft scope, not a universal four-test certificate.

Verification: PDF pp2–4 conditions, pp5–10 identities/routes and committee positions, pp10–16 testing table. The body does not establish clinical efficacy, a laboratory's accreditation, or a supplier's actual compliance.

## R5 — 503A and 503B are different operating routes

Direct source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fdc-act-provisions-apply-human-drug-compounding
Accessed 3 November 2026; undated public summary. High confidence on FDA's described federal framework; not exhaustive legal analysis.

503A applies to specified licensed pharmacists/pharmacies/federal facilities or physicians, with individual-patient prescription conditions and a CGMP exemption only when conditions are satisfied. 503B applies to registered outsourcing facilities, can operate without individual-patient prescriptions, and is not exempt from CGMP. Both bulk-substance routes require a valid COA and registered manufacturer, but differ in bulk eligibility. The table also distinguishes reporting, labeling, essentially-copy and transfer provisions. For 503B, another entity generally may not sell/transfer the compounded drug; the stated healthcare administration/prescription dispensing exceptions matter.

Verification: rendered lines 68–81 and 87–122, especially 105–110 and 119–122. This page does not make an independent testing company an outsourcing facility, permit distribution of any peptide, supersede state pharmacy law, or establish the startup's scope. Wedge implication: sell evidence to the accountable pharmacy; do not presume a reseller channel can be attached later without a separate legal structure.

## R6 — Bulk eligibility rules, independent of quality results

Direct source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/bulk-drug-substances-used-compounding
Accessed 3 November 2026, undated live summary. High confidence on described routes.

503A bulk eligibility follows applicable USP/NF monograph, otherwise component of an approved drug, otherwise the 503A bulks list. 503B bulk eligibility requires the relevant drug be on the shortage list at compounding/distribution/dispensing or the substance appear on the clinical-need 503B bulks list. Verification: rendered lines 72–87. These are statutory routes; interim enforcement policies must be analyzed separately.

A chemical test answers questions about a sample; it cannot supply missing legal eligibility. That is an inference from the rules, not a measurement of current buyer behavior. FDA's page points to knowing one's bulks supplier, supporting a provenance workstream alongside analysis, without proving buyers want a new intermediary.

## R7 — GLP-1 precedent: policy-conditioned demand can contract

Direct source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-alerts-and-statements/fda-clarifies-policies-compounders-national-glp-1-supply-begins-stabilize
Latest dated entry displayed 1 April 2026; accessed 3 November. High confidence on FDA's stated policy; not a live product-by-product shortage-database query.

The April entry says semaglutide and tirzepatide are absent from the 503B bulks and shortage lists. It reiterates 503A individual-patient prescription and essentially-copy restrictions, with documented prescriber significant-difference treatment. Adding another API does not automatically avoid copy rules. Historical entries record the end of shortage-dependent enforcement discretion and retain authority over quality/safety violations.

Verification: rendered lines 54–77 and 81–104. Do not describe all GLP-1 compounding as categorically prohibited or all personalization as permitted. The commercial inference is that regulatory exceptions can expire, so a startup should test demand across lawful categories and avoid economics requiring a temporary rule to persist.
