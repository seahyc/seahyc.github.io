# Peptide proof — research map

Research cut: 3 November 2026. US commercial wedge, with a Singapore-based founder possible; no Singapore clinical or import permission inferred. Phase 1 research, corrected for Phase 2. Public research, no customer interviews, laboratory experiments, orders or outreach.

## Founder decision

Should a founder build an independent testing and verification company for US compounding pharmacies, starting with buyer-controlled sampling, qualified-lab coordination and auditable ingredient-lot evidence for a narrowly lawful peptide workflow, without selling grey-market peptides?

Desired reader conclusion: fund a bounded paid discovery and qualification service, conditional on legal and insurance clearance. Do not buy a laboratory or launch a verified-supply storefront yet. An owned, scope-accredited lab is a possible later stage, not a claim the first service can inherit accreditation from subcontractors. Clinics are potential purchasers of pharmacy diligence, not substitutes for the pharmacy's quality authority. Consumers are a later, separately validated segment.

Human possibility: a pharmacist can trace a physical sample to its manufacturer, method, measured result and unresolved limitations before deciding what to dispense. This does not establish that the molecule works or is appropriate for a patient.

## Strongest bear case

This company could make unsafe consumption feel legitimate while owning neither product quality nor clinical evidence. Its report can be attached to a different lot; a hand-picked sample can pass; manufacture, storage, reconstitution and clinical use remain outside the tested vial. Buyers may want a marketing badge and suppression of failures, while serious pharmacies already have better laboratories and procurement controls. Established labs can bundle the proposed workflow. Consumers have free alternatives and may not pay. Regulatory swings can close the popular wedge; insurer exclusions or liability can make even a chemically competent service uneconomic. Adding supply revenue may undermine the very independence being sold.

Do not promise zero conflicts. Engineer disclosed, controlled conflicts: fixed buyer-paid fees, no sales commissions or supplier equity, buyer-controlled sample selection, separate technical approval, contractual raw-data rights, immutable corrections, explicit scope and dates, and no certificate that says safe to inject. Confidential pharmacy quality investigations need not be public; never repackage selectively released results as a representative public ranking.

## Falsifiers and next action

All numerical gates below are founder-chosen screening rules, not published industry benchmarks.

Owner: founder with retained US pharmacy counsel and senior analytical/quality lead. Timeframe: 90 days, with a legal gate before handling material.

Go to a repeat service only if two unrelated pharmacies each prepay at least $5,000 for a scoped pilot, three matched laboratory bids establish feasible scope, and at least two buyers place a paid follow-on order by day 90. On at least ten completed lot cases, require complete source/sample/method linkage, no unresolved critical discrepancy, and at least 30% collected contribution after direct lab, logistics, rerun reserve and fully costed review. Ten cases screen workflow; they cannot establish clinical safety or assay failure rates. A failure held and investigated counts as useful work, not as a result to erase.

Stop if counsel cannot identify a lawful scope, insurers will not cover that scope on affordable terms, buyers demand suppression or implied therapeutic endorsement, or direct incumbent purchasing solves the full job at acceptable cost. Stop laboratory capex if committed volume cannot support its full staffing, qualification and quality-system costs. Stop the consumer expansion if independent paid demand cannot cover sampling and acquisition. Stop supply expansion if impartial assessment and distribution duties cannot be reconciled.
