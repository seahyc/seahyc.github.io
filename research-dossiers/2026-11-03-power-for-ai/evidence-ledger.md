# Evidence ledger — Power for AI

Decision/access date: 2026-11-03. Status vocabulary: fact (including fact about an issuer disclosure), estimate, assumption, inference, scenario. Company statements are not independently metered outcomes. Confidence is in the narrowly stated claim, not delivery success. Sources dated after the cutoff are excluded. Unverified details in subsidiary source notes do not override the limitations below. Every proposed public material claim must map to an ID here; pending items require resolution or omission.

## E01 · Global demand and forecast are different objects
- Type: estimate / scenario; confidence high that IEA reports it, medium underlying model.
- Source/date: [IEA Energy and AI, April 2025](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary). Access 2026-11-03.
- Claim/proves: IEA estimates 415 TWh of global data-centre electricity in 2024 and forecasts around 945 TWh in 2030 in its Base Case. It spans 700–1,700 TWh in 2035 across cases. These are all data centres, not AI alone.
- Does not prove: Metered global census, certain growth, local capacity or startup revenue.
- Counterevidence/impact: High-efficiency and headwinds cases matter; label year and vintage. Do not present 2025 forecast as latest observation.

## E02 · Newer IEA observation and onsite constraint
- Type: estimate/inference by IEA; confidence medium-high.
- Source/date: [IEA, 16 April 2026](https://www.iea.org/news/data-centre-electricity-use-surged-in-2025-even-with-tightening-bottlenecks-driving-a-scramble-for-solutions).
- Claim/proves: IEA reports 17% growth in data-centre electricity demand in 2025, tightening turbine/transformer supply and many onsite-gas projects still early-stage. It describes load swings as an onsite-generator challenge and batteries as a useful complement.
- Does not prove: All onsite projects unbuilt or a universal engineering solution.
- Counterevidence/impact: Per-task efficiency improves while total use grows; distinguish demand, local peak and instantaneous ramp.

## E04 · Generation queue stock and attrition
- Type: fact about compiled dataset; confidence high within coverage.
- Source/date: [LBNL Queued Up 2026, June 2026, end-2025 data](https://emp.lbl.gov/queues/).
- Claim/proves: About 8,200 active projects: 1,312 GW generation and 749 GW storage. For 2000–2020 request capacity, 13% reached operation by end-2025 and 75% withdrew.
- Does not prove: AI load queue, already-built plants, equivalent firm output, universal five-year waiting time, or capacity available to buy.
- Counterevidence/impact: Active capacity fell 10% year on year; reforms and withdrawals mean queue growth is not inevitable. Cohort outcomes are not a probability for an individual current site.
