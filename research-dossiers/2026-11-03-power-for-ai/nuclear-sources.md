## Core distinction

**Criticality is not the same milestone as power generation.** A zero-power fueled criticality demonstration establishes a self-sustaining chain reaction in a controlled test, but does not demonstrate useful electricity output, sustained full-power operation, commercial operation, or an NRC operating license. A construction permit, DOE test authorization, completed safety review, and commercial power delivery are separate statuses. DOE’s own descriptions label several milestone tests “zero-power fueled criticality demonstrations.”

## DOE Reactor Pilot Program: deadline and actual result

- **Original target:** DOE’s June 18, 2025 program announcement says it sought at least three advanced reactors to achieve criticality by July 4, 2026, using DOE authorization to test outside national laboratories. The objective stated is criticality, not commercial electricity delivery.
- **Result:** DOE’s July 25, 2026 fact sheet says the target was exceeded: four reactors achieved criticality by the deadline—Antares Nuclear Mark-0 (June 4), Valar Atomics Ward 250 (June 18), Deployable Energy Unity (June 30), and Aalo Atomics Aalo-X (July 4). DOE’s July 1 release confirms Unity was the third and says it completed a zero-power fueled demonstration; its July 6 release describes Aalo-X likewise. DOE therefore reports the milestone exceeded; the original outside-laboratories condition is unresolved against this INL-inclusive tally.
- **Scope boundary:** These were test reactor demonstrations. DOE does not claim that the four events themselves delivered commercial electricity. For Antares specifically, the DOE July fact sheet discusses subsequent electricity production in 2027 and following years; Aalo says its separate 10 MWe follow-on Project Ascension was planned to power an on-site data center in 2027. Those are forward plans, not delivered power at the cutoff.

## Company checks

### Valar Atomics — spelling and status

The company’s name is **Valar Atomics** (not “Valor”); its reactor is styled **Ward 250** on the official page. DOE’s June 18 release confirms Ward 250 completed a **zero-power fueled criticality demonstration** at the Utah San Rafael Energy Lab. Valar’s June 28 update says Ward 250 is an HTGR using TRISO fuel and pressurized helium, with a test authorization of 30 effective full-power days at 100 kWt and up to one day at 250 kWt. The source supports test reactor operation and a criticality milestone; it does not establish commercial electricity delivery or an operating commercial plant.

Primary sources: [DOE, June 18, 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-second-advanced-reactor-achieving-criticality); [Valar, Ward 250](https://www.valaratomics.com/ward-250); [Valar, “Ward 250 By the Numbers,” June 28, 2026](https://www.valaratomics.com/docs/Ward-250-By-the-Numbers).

### Oklo

Oklo’s **Groves Isotope Test Reactor** achieved first criticality on August 5, 2026, according to Oklo’s August 6 company announcement, following DOE authorization. The announcement calls it a low-power isotope test reactor, and says its operational foundation is for future isotope production. This is not evidence that its commercial Aurora power plant is generating electricity. In its June 30, 2026 SEC filing, Oklo reported DOE approval of Aurora-INL’s Preliminary Documented Safety Analysis (June 11) and described Aurora as in DOE authorization/development; it also described Meta’s January 5 prepayment arrangement to advance the proposed 1.2 GW Pike County, Ohio campus. The agreement is a financing/development milestone, not delivered energy. Meta’s January 9 announcement describes the campus as potentially online as early as 2030.

Primary sources: [Oklo, Groves first criticality, Aug. 6, 2026](https://oklo.com/newsroom/oklos-groves-reactor-achieves-first-criticality-in-under-a-year); [Oklo 2026 Q2 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026054571/oklo-20260630.htm); [Meta, Jan. 9, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/meta-nuclear-energy-projects-power-american-ai-leadership/).

### Kairos Power / Google

NRC’s Hermes 2 application page says NRC issued Kairos construction permits on November 21, 2024 for two 35 MWth test reactors. Kairos broke ground on Hermes 2 in April 2026, calling it its first power-producing plant and stating up to 50 MWe would go to the TVA grid. Kairos’s current Google page describes the binding TVA power purchase agreement, Hermes 2 starting in 2030, and the broader fleet goal of up to 500 MWe by 2035. These are future delivery targets. Hermes 2 is a construction-permitted demonstration plant; no evidence reviewed here says it had reached criticality or was supplying power by the cutoff. Hermes 1 is a separate low-power demonstration reactor; Kairos’s March 2026 status page placed it under construction and said it “could see action in 2028.”

Primary sources: [NRC Hermes 2 project page](https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/non-power-facilities/new-facility-licensing/hermes-2-kairos-application); [Kairos, Hermes 2 groundbreaking, Apr. 17, 2026](https://www.kairospower.com/updates/kairos-power-breaks-ground-on-hermes-2-demonstration-plant); [Kairos / Google agreement](https://www.kairospower.com/google); [Kairos iterative development/status](https://www.kairospower.com/iterative-development); [Kairos, March 26, 2026](https://www.kairospower.com/updates/baseball-and-kairos-power-have-this-one-thing-in-common).

### X-energy / Amazon

X-energy’s Xe-100 had no criticality milestone identified in the primary materials reviewed. As of Oct. 30, 2026, X-energy reported the NRC had completed its safety review for the proposed four-unit Long Mott project with Dow; the NRC Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards supported issuing a construction permit, but the company said the project was moving to the final stages of permit review. A safety review is not the permit, construction completion, criticality, or power delivery.

Amazon’s and X-energy’s agreements concern projects, not current reactor output. Their Aug. 2025 announcement targeted more than 5 GW across US projects by 2039. Their Oct. 19, 2026 announcement with AEP says the Indiana proposal is a 12-unit/960 MW project in feasibility work under a Joint Development Agreement; it explicitly lists risk that feasibility may not support proceeding and permits, financing, and execution remain necessary. Cascade in Washington is likewise a proposed 4–12 unit project. X-energy describes Long Mott as proposed; its submitted Construction Permit Application was still under NRC review at cutoff.

Primary sources: [X-energy, NRC safety review, Oct. 30, 2026](https://x-energy.com/news/nrc-completes-landmark-safety-review-of-x-energys-xe-100-smr); [X-energy / Amazon / AEP, Oct. 19, 2026](https://investors.x-energy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/x-energy-amazon-american-electric-power-partner-bring-smr); [X-energy / Amazon / KHNP / Doosan, Aug. 25, 2025](https://x-energy.com/news/x-energy-amazon-korea-hydro-amp-nuclear-power-and-doosan-enerbility-announce-partnership-to-scale-advanced-nuclear-energy-for-ai-infrastructure/); [X-energy, deployments](https://x-energy.com/deployments/); [X-energy, regulatory milestones](https://x-energy.com/regulatory-milestones/).

### Radiant

Radiant’s **Kaleidos** is a 1 MWe transportable microreactor. It shipped to Idaho National Laboratory in August 2026 for testing. On Oct. 29, Radiant said DOE had approved its Documented Safety Analysis, allowing it to advance to a DOE Readiness Review—the next step before nuclear operations can begin. Therefore, as of cutoff, the official statement indicates it had **not yet started fueled operations or achieved criticality**. The company described future test phases (criticality through 60 effective full-power days and a 150-hour full-power run), and first commercial deliveries to military customers in 2028 as plans. Its own August source had targeted a Q3 test milestone, but the Oct. 29 update establishes that readiness review still remained.

Primary sources: [Radiant, Kaleidos shipment, Aug. 12/13, 2026](https://radiantnuclear.com/news/kaleidos-has-left-the-building); [Radiant, DSA approval, Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.radiantnuclear.com/news/radiant-secure-doe-approval-for-full-power-operations).

### Last Energy

Last Energy’s press release index, accessed Nov. 3, lists an October 2026 DOE approval of the Documented Safety Analysis for its Texas A&M–RELLIS pilot reactor, after May 2026 approval of the Preliminary DSA. The PWR-5 pilot is described as a 5 MWe scaled version of its proposed PWR-20, intended to demonstrate low-power criticality and electricity generation. The index does not establish that it had achieved criticality by cutoff. Separately, UK regulator ONR says its 2025 preliminary design review was completed and Last Energy was preparing an application for a nuclear site licence; a PDR is not a site licence or permission to construct. Treat PWR-20 commercial capacities and deployments as proposed.

Primary sources: [Last Energy press releases](https://www.lastenergy.com/news-press-categories/press-release); [UK ONR, licensing status](https://www.onr.org.uk/our-work/what-we-regulate/new-reactors/licensing-of-new-reactors); [UK ONR, PDR summary](https://www.onr.org.uk/generic-design-assessment/early-regulatory-engagement-on-new-nuclear-projects/last-energy-pdr-summary-report).

### Aalo Atomics

DOE confirms Aalo-X achieved a zero-power fueled criticality demonstration at INL on July 4, 2026, the fourth DOE-authorized design by the goal date. Aalo’s own update distinguishes its full-scale core **critical test reactor** from its next step: a 10 MWe power reactor, then 50 MWe Aalo Pods. Its Project Ascension 10 MWe reactor was planned to generate electricity for an on-site data center in 2027. These stages should not be collapsed into “Aalo is already powering AI.”

Primary sources: [DOE, July 6, 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal); [Aalo, July 4, 2026](https://www.aalo.com/post/aalo-achieves-first-criticality).

### NuScale

NuScale has a distinct design-approval milestone among the named peers: company and NRC sources show NRC design approvals for the 50 MWe design and Standard Design Approval for its uprated 77 MWe module (May 29, 2025). That is a design review status, not a construction or operating license for a project and not evidence of a deployed NuScale generating plant. NuScale’s company history lists a new six-module Standard Design Approval application accepted by NRC in 2026. NuScale is not a DOE July 4 pilot criticality result in the sources reviewed.

Primary sources: [NRC, NuScale 77 MWe design approval, May 29, 2025](https://www.nrc.gov/node/2151866); [NuScale, 77 MWe design approval](https://www.nuscalepower.com/press-releases/2025/nuscale-powers-small-modular-reactor-smr-achieves-standard-design-approval-from-us-nuclear-regulatory-commission-for-77-mwe); [NuScale company history](https://www.nuscalepower.com/about/history).

## Utility/customer agreements in the AI context

- **Google–Kairos/TVA:** Binding PPA is between Kairos and TVA; up to 50 MWe from Hermes 2 is intended for the TVA grid and Google data centers, with service expected from Hermes 2 in 2030. Google’s broader Kairos fleet agreement targets up to 500 MWe by 2035. This is future supply, not present generation.
- **Amazon–X-energy:** Amazon invested in X-energy and is partnering on planned deployments. The latest reviewed Indiana agreement (Oct. 19, 2026) is feasibility work for a proposed 960 MW project, with Amazon supplying development capital. The separate Cascade Washington project is also planned. The announced 5+ GW is an ambition/portfolio target through 2039, not operating capacity.
- **Meta:** Meta’s Jan. 9, 2026 announcement combines existing-fleet support and advanced-reactor development. It says its Oklo Ohio campus may come online as early as 2030; TerraPower’s first two Natrium units are targeted for delivery as early as 2032, with additional units targeted by 2035. Vistra and Constellation agreements support existing plants and uprates. These commitments are not all new generation, and the combined headline capacity should not be represented as new reactors already serving Meta. Meta’s 2025 Constellation deal covers the existing Clinton plant starting 2027.

Primary sources: [Kairos / Google](https://www.kairospower.com/google); [X-energy / AEP / Amazon](https://investors.x-energy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/x-energy-amazon-american-electric-power-partner-bring-smr); [Meta, Jan. 9, 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/meta-nuclear-energy-projects-power-american-ai-leadership/); [Meta / Constellation, June 3, 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/06/meta-constellation-partner-clean-energy-project/).

## Evidence gaps / not independently verified here

- No primary source reviewed substantiates electricity delivery by Ward 250, Groves, Aalo-X, Hermes 2, Long Mott, Kaleidos, Last Energy PWR-5/PWR-20, or NuScale by the cutoff.
- Radiant’s Oct. 29 release explicitly places a DOE Readiness Review before beginning nuclear operations; therefore it supersedes its earlier Q3 target as evidence of actual status.
- Last Energy’s October DSA approval was found in its official press-release index, but the underlying release page was not accessible through the web reader. I therefore report only the index statement and do not infer authorization, fueling, or criticality.
- The NRC Hermes 2 page was discoverable and opened via its official URL in search, but an attempted second fetch returned 403. The permit facts above are from the NRC page content available in the initial official-source result; Kairos’s own pages independently support the project description and construction progress.
- Valar’s June 28 reactor details and company status come from a company-authored post; DOE independently confirms its criticality date and location. Commercial-scale economics and readiness are not established by those sources.
- No inference is made from company pipeline totals to contracted, permitted, financed, under-construction, or delivered megawatts unless the cited source says so.
