# Power for AI — research map

Decision date: 3 November 2026. Phase 1 only. US first; Singapore as a contrasting market. No procurement, contact, site operation or publication authorized by this research.

## Founder decision and desired conclusion
Should a founder build a power-readiness engineering and workflow company for independent data-centre developers, starting with a verified equipment-and-energisation plan for an already-controlled site, rather than manufacturing reactors, owning generation, or promising to remove a grid queue?

Initial hypothesis, to be revised after source collection: sell a narrow, engineer-reviewed deliverable that reconciles utility service milestones, transformer/switchgear specifications and firm vendor slots. The customer buys a credible path to an energised first phase. Software can improve coordination; it cannot create transmission capacity or confer a permit. Desired conclusion: distinguish the best science from the best initial company boundary, and commit only to a paid test of the latter.

## Core assumptions — explicitly unverified
- Developers lose enough money to power delays that an independent, decision-grade plan has budget.
- At least some delays arise from preventable specification/procurement/sequence mistakes rather than unavoidable system work.
- Licensed engineering partners and vendors will provide usable records without the startup taking equipment inventory or performance guarantees.
- A reusable workflow exists across sites; general consulting is a plausible outcome if it does not.
- US evidence does not transfer automatically to Singapore regulation, fuel, land or tariffs.

## Strongest bear case
AI demand forecasts can overstate realised load. Incumbent manufacturers add capacity, utilities reform queues, hyperscalers internalise procurement and experienced EPCs already coordinate the whole job. Even if delays are severe, the binding constraint may be copper, land, fuel, manufacturing, permission or transmission construction. An AI workflow adds little if it merely draws a better schedule around immovable constraints. Liability, long sales cycles and bespoke engineering can consume all apparent software margin.

## Initial falsifiers and action
Founder owns a 90-day discovery and paid-pilot gate: request permissioned histories from developers and an independent electrical engineer; measure preventable rework, verified critical-path change and paid willingness. Thresholds and economics will be specified after source collection; none are industry benchmarks. No interviews have yet occurred.

## Evidence-updated conclusion

**Provisional allocation: attack equipment-readiness and energisation coordination for the developer, with an independent electrical engineer in the loop. Start as a bounded paid service and earn the right to turn recurring work into software.** E06/E08 establish specification/assurance friction, E04/E05 establish system-process complexity, E11 establishes existing software competition. None establishes the proposed buyer's willingness to pay. This is a research-backed testable wedge, not a finding of product-market fit.

A founder with reactor physics, fuel, licensing, nuclear operations and patient capital could reasonably choose a reactor company. That is a different team and capital mandate. These sources cannot rank peer cost curves or prove that Valar's manufacturing philosophy will win. A general software founding team should not infer access to a reactor business from accelerated test milestones.

## Wedge specification and actors

Initial prospecting segment (assumption): US independent/colocation developers that control a site, have a named utility process and phased load plan, and lack a hyperscaler's procurement team. Prospecting first phase is 10–50 MW; not an observed market-size band. Sponsor: head of development/power; budget owner and engineer-of-record must be identified rather than assumed. Utility/transmission provider accepts service conditions; vendor owns quote and equipment slot; independent qualified engineer reviews equipment and protection compatibility; EPC installs/commissions. These actors have different authority.

Deliverable: dated evidence pack with single-line diagram and load-ramp assumptions supplied/reviewed by responsible engineers; utility correspondence and service/curtailment terms; required studies/permits and deposits; transformer and switchgear requirements and approved alternates; manufacturer-confirmed quotes/slot expiry/testing/transport milestones; dependencies, owner and decision for each critical-path item. Compare grid-only, phased load/flexible connection and qualified onsite partner. Every date carries issuer, evidence, conditions and expiry. Document the explicit blocker even when no acceleration is possible.

Own the reconciliation and audit trail. Partner for engineering review and authority approvals. Do not claim to certify equipment, guarantee energisation, make autonomous grid studies, take fuel/merchant-price risk, carry inventory or promise reactor delivery. Public information cannot confirm a usable manufacturer slot; access to current private evidence is part of the test.

## What the headline omits

- A GW in a queue is a request; generator/storage queue is not a large-load queue. MWh/year, peak MW, ramp MW/s and firm capacity answer different questions. Cannot add battery MW to generation output.
- A PPA may support existing generation, restart, uprate or an unbuilt plant. Financial procurement is not proof of physical delivery to one campus.
- Data-centre IT MW excludes facility overhead; onsite gross nameplate excludes auxiliary loads, ambient derating, maintenance and redundancy. Public numbers rarely share the same boundary.
- Onsite plants can bypass some grid dependence while inheriting gas network, emissions, cooling, protection and operating obligations. Batteries buffer, but need charge energy.
- Physical critical path is the maximum of dependent readiness dates, not the sum of exciting announcements. Advancing an item off the critical path creates no energisation gain.
- Better software has no assured take rate on the customer's project capex. Paid discovery and observed fee acceptance are required. A good engineering service is not automatically a scalable venture.

## Falsifiers and next action — proposed gates, not industry norms

Owner: founder, with independent qualified electrical engineer as technical reviewer. Timeframe: 90 days from authorized customer access, not from publication. No outreach has been sent.

Days 1–30: interview 12 decision makers across at least four independent developers; inspect permissioned histories of six delayed projects including best incumbent workflow and cancellation cases. Stop broad build if fewer than three histories show a material avoidable specification/procurement/coordination error accepted by the customer's engineer, or if project data cannot be lawfully shared. Do not label general transmission construction as software-addressable delay.

Days 31–60: offer three bounded paid pilots with contract scope and liability reviewed. Compare against existing engineer/EPC/software configuration. Proceed only if at least two pay, the responsible engineer accepts the deliverable without unresolved critical compatibility errors, and customer confirms an actionable critical-path improvement or a costly mistaken commitment avoided. Target 30 calendar days of preventable delay as a screening metric, not a promised saving; a forecast schedule change is not an actual saved day.

Days 61–90: measure actual collected fee, engineering/operations/CAC hours, rework, insurance/legal burden, unique versus reusable work and one follow-on order. For economic go: measured after-CAC contribution at least $25,000/package and credible capacity to close/deliver at least 12/year would cover the illustrative $300,000 central overhead; stronger buffer needed before hiring. Those dollar thresholds come from E30 assumptions and must be replaced with actual cost commitments. Stop or remain a consultancy if reusable evidence workflow is absent or incumbents satisfy the job cheaper. Never accept unsafe substitution to meet an acceleration metric.

## Conditional 12–24 month attack plan for later narrative

Months 0–3: establish source access, paid comparative pilot and authority boundaries. Months 4–6: repeat same deliverable with actual collections and controlled scope. Months 7–12: automate repeated evidence reconciliation only after multiple sites show shared work; integrate with incumbent studies/workflow rather than replacing trust. Months 13–24: expand to one second utility region or an onsite-partner evaluation module after measured repeat contribution; do not simultaneously enter generation ownership or nuclear development. Each expansion needs new tariff/engineering validation. All dates are proposed sequence, not sales or regulatory forecasts.
