Verbatim:
> Fighting abuse & spam on a consumer app is a perpetual cat-and-mouse game:
>
> Every solution you create is a bandaid for 3-12 months -- until the adversaries find a workaround. When money is involved, people are tenacious to circumvent your patch and keep the money flowing.
>
> While large language models have made attacks more intractable, there are tools you can build to protect your product:
>
> (1) An in-house Risk Scoring stack that assigns a 0-100 score of likelihood someone is a spammer or bot. This should be informed by machine learning, IP & email reputation data, device telemetry, among many other signals.
>
> (2) A near-bulletproof method to "checkpoint" your highest risk users, such as liveness verification or phone number auth. This will have to be reinvented every year (e.g., CAPTCHA tests are obsolete now with today's models).
>
> As new attacks emerge, you can move the risk threshold lower to checkpoint more people. However, you want to be cautious so you don't add unnecessary friction for too many people and impact usage.

Author's own reply (to @CryptoWendyO, who wrote "Unless you KYC all users"):

What it proves: a senior practitioner's stated view of the problem shape (short-lived fixes, LLMs worsening attacks, in-house risk scoring + checkpoints, CAPTCHA obsolescence, KYC trade-off). What it does not prove: market size, willingness to pay for a vendor (he recommends in-house), or that the 3–12 month figure is measured rather than experiential.
