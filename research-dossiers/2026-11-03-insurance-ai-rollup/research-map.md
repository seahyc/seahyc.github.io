# Research map — insurance-ai-rollup
As of 3 November 2026. Phase 1 only. Public-source research and original planning scenarios; no interviews, offers, acquisitions or customer tests performed.

## Founder decision and working answer
Should a founder build an AI-enabled commercial insurance broker platform for Asian mid-market businesses, starting with a Singapore specialty brokerage serving regional logistics/manufacturing clients, and add claims advocacy and separately mandated recovery?

**Conditional route choice:** build the broker operating company, beginning with a permissioned workflow/claims service alongside one licensed Singapore anchor, then acquire only after underwriting its existing book and keeping its producers. Start in Singapore; Hong Kong and Taiwan are conditional expansions, not simultaneous launches. Use software to improve owned delivery and buy available tools before inventing a suite. Claims advocacy is part of the customer promise; insurer subrogation is a distinct, separately priced business only where rights and economics support it. Do not underwrite acquisitions on recovery fees, retention uplift, AI margin expansion or a larger resale multiple.

Desired reader conclusion: an attractive client experience is plausible—one accountable broker that knows the business at renewal and when a loss occurs. The economic asset is renewable client/producer relationships and insurer access. AI document handling is an instrument. The three-country institutional-scale case remains unproved because the independently owned, commercially relevant, willing-to-sell earnings pool has not been measured. Wider APAC distribution is the preferred scale hypothesis; MGA/program and wholesale/reinsurance are optional capabilities requiring distinct talent, authority and carrier trust, not mandatory labels or automatic higher margins.

Alternative: a US recovery agency has a clearer insurer/plan payer but needs legal rights, data access, expert casework and collection finance in a crowded established category. Narrow broker software is cheaper to test but faces existing workflow vendors and limited local buyer budgets. Neither is ruled out; the route choice changes if the acquisition gates fail and a service/software pilot demonstrates stronger paid demand.

## Actors and incentives
- Corporate risk/finance manager: reliable placement, clear exclusions, recoverable losses and prompt cash; may switch brokers despite acquisition agreements.
- Producer/broker owner: personal relationships, autonomy, fair proceeds, a funded succession and credible earnout; retention depends on behavior, not just contracts.
- Insurer/underwriter: quality submissions, profitable risk, broker creditworthiness and clean client-money handling; owes recovery proceeds to the appropriate rights holder, not the platform.
- Claims advocate / adjuster / lawyer: different client, licence, mandate and professional obligations; conflicts must be identified before any fee.
- Sponsor/lender: recurring normalized earnings, leverage headroom, controls, management continuity and eventual buyer liquidity.
- Founder: salary initially; common equity or vesting management incentive later. Company enterprise value is not personal cash.

## Strongest bear case
This may be a small-market search fund wearing a technology multiple. Multinationals can serve regional commercial accounts and buy the same AI. Local broker counts overstate the available book; attractive sellers know consolidation prices. Each additional country adds licensed people, compliance and client-money plumbing. Centralization can lose the very producers whose relationships were purchased. Claims advocacy may be bundled already, while insurer subrogation rights sit outside the broker. A sponsor can provide money and capture most of the equity. A leveraged exit can be delayed by credit markets or strategic buyers' appetite even when operations improve.

## Falsifiers and next action
Stop the roll-up thesis if no three diligence-ready sellers emerge within 90 days; if cash earnings require unproved AI/recovery uplift to service debt; if normalized renewal revenue falls below 95% in the first full measured renewal cohort; if important carrier relationships cannot survive control changes; or if counsel cannot establish a workable licensed operating and client-money structure. These are proposed management gates, not observed outcomes or regulator timelines.

Next action: founder + experienced commercial broking operator, 90 days, capped USD150,000 research/pilot budget (execution-path.md). Produce the owner/earnings map, three pilot commitments and a consented anchor data room. Go only with a retained accountable operator, downside cash debt-service coverage >=1.5x under actual financing terms, clean money reconciliation and a credible non-AI acquisition case. Otherwise retain the best demonstrated fee service, test a small software sale, or stop. No outbound activity is authorized by producing this dossier.
