# Public transaction and operator evidence — insurance AI rollup

Research cutoff/access: 3 November 2026. Primary pages below were opened, not accepted solely from search snippets. Read Fieldbook SKILL.md and research-method.md before research. No git commands; public sources only.

## Conclusions calibrated to evidence

- Consolidation and institutional capital access are established facts. They do not demonstrate that a new AI rollup can acquire cheaply, retain producers, improve organic growth, or finance acquisitions on equivalent terms.
- Aon–USI is real: announced 31 August 2026. Latest verified 28 October release still describes it as pending. Do not call completed as of this research cutoff without a subsequent closing release.
- Keep valuation bases separate: Aon’s tax-adjusted/synergized adjusted EBITDA multiple, TIH’s core EBITDA multiple, MarshBerry’s platform base/maximum earnout multiples, and preferred/common funding valuations are not comparable denominators or securities.
- Retaining producers and domain experts is a core integration question. Employee shareholding and earnouts are observed mechanisms, not evidence of a measured causal retention benefit.

## Next diligence that could change the founder decision

1. Obtain three real seller offers in one jurisdiction; reconcile cash at close, rollover, contingent payments, net debt, working capital and EBITDA addbacks. Reject comparison based on headline multiples.
2. Ask lenders for a written term sheet including covenants and treatment of earnouts, producer departures and AI/security expenditure; institutional incumbents’ financing does not establish startup bankability.
3. Obtain cohort-level producer/client retention at 12/24/36 months, compensation changes and client ownership restrictions. Share schemes and promises are not retention outcomes.
4. Measure one repeated broker workflow before acquisition: accepted cases/hour, error/rework rate, expert review time, client outcome and actual cash margin. Savings must include systems/data cleanup and expert supervision.
5. For Asian claims advocacy, validate buyer, regulated role, permitted fee model, bundled incumbents and legal escalation with local counsel and paying customers. Evidence presently supports service existence, not an unoccupied profitable wedge.
