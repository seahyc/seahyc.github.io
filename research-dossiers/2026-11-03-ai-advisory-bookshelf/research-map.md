# AI advisory bookshelf — phase 1 research map
Dated 3 November 2026. Public evidence, not customer validation. Phase 1 only; no article, visuals, product trial, outreach or publication. Initial branches: advice demand, expert fidelity, rights, differentiation, retention and economics. Evidence collection revised the disagreement branch after finding Delphi's existing multi-perspective pages.

## Founder decision
Should a founder build a curated, cited advisory bookshelf delivered through MCP into the AI people already use, for bootstrapped B2B founders, starting with recurring pricing/packaging decisions and a source-linked decision journal?

The full concept includes problem-specific experts/passages, the user's own books, disagreement by design, and an evidence record of what advisers said versus what they actually did. The initial wedge deliberately uses permitted public material and opt-in personal highlights; book licensing is a separate gate. Customer, workflow and price are hypotheses, not findings. Initial research/customer access could be English-speaking Singapore/global founders; there is no country-specific demand estimate. The model uses USD for comparison, without currency conversion.

## Desired reader conclusion
A useful personal tool is plausible; a venture-sized standalone layer is unproven and currently a poor allocation of YC's time. Existing products occupy retrieval, purchased-book context, personas AND explicit comparison. Run a small paid comparison only if cheap access to target customers already exists. Do not read this as proof that the market is intrinsically capped: willingness to pay, reachable customer volume and retention are missing. The correct present decision is no venture commitment; conditional side project or do nothing.

## Human-scale possibility and mechanism
A founder asks about changing a pricing model inside their existing AI. The bookshelf returns attributable passages, conflicting applicability conditions, missing facts, and a dated case where the adviser made a relevant decision. The user records their own assumptions, choice, expected result and review date. Later they revisit the evidence and outcome. This is a proposed experience, not an implementation.

MCP supplies tools/context; it does not guarantee that the host calls the tool or preserves citations and uncertainty. Candidate pipeline: classify the decision and ask for missing context → retrieve from a rights-tagged corpus → open original context → separate quotation, summary and new inference → compare genuinely different conditional recommendations → retrieve timestamped action records → return evidence objects to host → user approves a decision record → follow up on outcome. Outcome journaling is optional, consented and exportable. A host answer must never be presented as an expert's original words.

## Actors, incentives and capture
- User: wants better decisions with less reading/setup; already pays for an AI and may prefer a simple saved prompt.
- Founder: needs recurring paid use and cheap distribution; bibliographic richness alone has no renewal evidence.
- Expert/author/publisher: may want reach, sales and control; may object to unauthorised attribution or substitution.
- Host AI/lab: owns conversation and billing relationship; can bundle or improve advice, making the layer easier to replace.
- Readwise/Google/Delphi/open projects: already have relevant source access, distribution or content supply. Their availability is documented; superiority is not tested.

## Core assumptions
A1 A narrow class of decisions recurs often enough to justify another subscription.
A2 Curated counterarguments outperform both default AI and AI plus a user's existing library.
A3 Users value provenance and prospective outcome records enough to return after novelty.
A4 Corpus rights, source context and host behaviour can be handled at modest recurring cost.
A5 A repeatable distribution channel reaches buyers at a contribution-compatible acquisition cost.
A6 Evidence capture produces learning users cannot reproduce with a prompt and document. None of A1–A6 is validated commercially.

## Strongest bear case
The user wants a better answer, not a bookshelf. Their host can already reason over supplied text, Readwise exposes their library, Google has publisher-backed purchased books, Delphi has real-person supply and comparative pages, and brains.ceo offers a free cited MCP. More sophisticated retrieval is easy to substitute; a founder pays for their AI first. A slow, rights-sensitive human editorial layer may be the only differentiator but also the cost centre. Advice can feel useful while failing to improve decisions. Pricing decisions may be too infrequent for retention. A polished library can earn modest subscription revenue and still be an inferior use of YC's scarce founder time. No evidence currently overcomes this case.

## Falsifiers and stop rules (proposed thresholds, not empirical benchmarks)
1. Quality: no meaningful advantage against the strongest accessible substitute with equal user context → stop generic bookshelf thesis.
2. Attribution: any fabricated quote or material unsupported attribution in the launch audit → pause release, repair and re-audit. Zero observed errors is a screening result, not universal assurance.
3. Rights: core advantage requires unavailable or uneconomic permissions → remove affected corpus; stop if advantage disappears.
4. Demand: interview enthusiasm without actual payments/renewals → no venture commitment.
5. Economics: support, editorial work or churn replacement makes contribution nonpositive → reprice/narrow once; otherwise stop paid expansion.
6. Defensibility: users reproduce the useful result using incumbent prompts/library without continued product use → side-project utility only.

## Next action: bounded validation before building a company
Owner: founder (YC or delegated operator), plus one independent source reviewer. Nothing below has been executed.
- Days 1–14: recruit 12 qualified founders already using AI; inspect their last two pricing/packaging decisions and incumbent workflows. Do not send unsolicited messages as part of this dossier. Continue only if at least 6 have a real upcoming decision and agree to a bounded paid trial, rather than hypothetical interest.
- Days 15–45: compare three arms: existing AI with an explicit challenge-the-premise prompt; AI + existing library/Readwise or Google where available; same AI/context + curated evidence pack. Use 24 anonymised decisions across the cohort as a feasibility screen, counterbalance order, blind arm identity for independent review. Same model/version and context budget; log actual tool calls, fallback and all editorial minutes. These sample sizes are chosen budget caps, not power calculations.
- Score source entailment, missed constraints, viable alternatives and usable next actions with a preregistered rubric; user preference is separate from expert assessment. Gate: curated arm wins at least 16/24 paired decisions against the stronger comparator with no material attribution failures. This is a chosen hurdle; overlapping decisions/users mean no population-level significance claim.
- Demand gate by day 75: at least 6/12 pay USD19 for the first month and at least 4 renew once without a bundled consulting service; at least 4 return for a second independent decision. Refunds do not count as retained revenue. Failure → archive or maintain only as an unpaid tool. Passing → capped side business experiment, not venture proof.
- Operational gate: median recurring support <=4 min/payer/month, shared editorial work <= scenario cash budget, no permission gap; report tail support and founder time too. Observe actual net contribution after acquisition/churn, not gross margin alone.

## Conditional 12–24 month path
0–3 months: run the above screen; cap new engineering to source-object format, comparison harness and exportable journal. Do not build a new chat app.
3–6 months, only after payment gate: one domain, permissioned source list, original-context audit, user-controlled library connector and renewal cohorts. Measure retention beyond the launch period.
6–12 months: test whether opt-in decision records improve repeat use; build longitudinal public case files with failed examples and explicit missingness. Add one channel only after acquisition contribution works.
12–24 months: expand to an adjacent recurring decision only with paid demand and reusable editorial process. Consider a venture only with independently observed cohort retention, an acquisition channel, positive fully loaded contribution and paid expansion. Otherwise keep a modest product or stop. No forecast of meeting these gates.

## What the headline omits
Guidance shares are not buyer counts. A retrieved citation is not an endorsement or faithful quotation. Agreement with experts is not good real-world outcomes. Owned copies are not the same as transferable ingestion/display rights. Announced funding is not paid demand. A low scraper bill excludes subscriptions, human audit and permissions. Disagreement already exists in competing products. Neither public successes nor a user's self-report establish that an expert's advice caused an outcome.
