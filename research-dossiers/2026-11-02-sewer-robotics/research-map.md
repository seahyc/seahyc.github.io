# Sewer robotics and AI — internal research dossier
Dated 2 November 2026. Public-evidence research, not customer validation. No Gemini pass requested or used.

## Founder decision
Should a founder build a robotics-enabled inspection company for sewer CCTV contractors, starting with a Singapore-specific field-to-accepted-report workflow on existing equipment?
Desired conclusion: fund a bounded field experiment; do not fund a new general-purpose crawler yet. A local service could be useful without being venture-scale. Geography: Singapore beachhead; US/UK/EU comparisons are competition and technical context, not transferable procurement rules. 12–24 month conditional plan.

## Hypothesis tree
1. Quality failures cause costly revisits. Evidence: PUB requires clear cleaned inspection and rejects non-compliance. Counter: no public rejection rate or cost. Test rejected-job logs and time-motion observation.
2. AI can reduce review and catch missing capture. Evidence: Sewer-ML, SewerAI and Vapar. Counter: QAI already covers overlapping workflow, domain shift and residual human review. Test against purchased incumbent + manual workflow on held-out jobs.
3. Field productivity rather than model accuracy creates durable value. Mechanism: permits, cleaning, capture, review, submission, recovery. Counter: delays may be external or incumbents may solve it. Test accepted metres/crew-day and all-in cost, including failures.
4. A custom robot unlocks a repeated blocked task. PUB sampling shows need; 24 historical deployments/year undermines stand-alone scale. Test paid repeat demand and reusable payload before custom locomotion.
5. Geographic expansion can scale. Missing: named customers, local standards, procurement and pricing. Test independent second jurisdiction partner; do not multiply Singapore stock into TAM.

## Strongest bear case
Existing crawlers plus established AI vendors may solve the affordable part. Cleaning, access and traffic dominate; software creates no extra paid capacity. Contractors may lack rights to reuse footage. Municipal budgets may pay per completed contract, giving contractors no upside from speed. The startup inherits service logistics and recovery risk while the incumbent owns data workflows. A profitable contractor is not automatically a high-growth technology company.

## Wedge and disproof
Proposed buyer: registered CCTV contractor, initially job owner/operations lead. Product hypothesis: offline-capable capture checks, asset/distance linkage, uncertainty flags and standards-specific report packaging with competent human sign-off. Contractual buyer acceptance remains outside the tool's control. Earn advantage through local integration and measurable fewer revisits; not a novel QA category. Compare licensed incumbent software before building. Stop if 30 matched paid crew-days do not reduce full cost per accepted metre by 20% versus existing best available workflow, with no observed increase in critical misses or acceptance failures. This sample is a screening gate, not safety certification; continue larger blinded validation before autonomy claims.

## Next experiment
Founder + contractor operations lead, 90 days: ten contractor/asset-owner interviews and permissioned rejected-job log review; two participating contractors; matched jobs split by diameter, water, camera, material, accessibility and task; independent competent adjudicator. Obtain quotations and public-access permissions through contractor. No contact, purchase, field deployment or submission performed by this research.
