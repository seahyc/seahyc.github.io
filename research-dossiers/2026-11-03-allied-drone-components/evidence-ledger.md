As of 3 November 2026. All URLs accessed on that date unless stated. Facts about vendor offers mean the supplier publishes the claim; they do not establish audited origin, inventory, price equivalence or achieved performance. No customer evidence has been collected. Companion supplier-notes.md supplies detailed P1–P11 source records, including dates, counterevidence and limits.

## E01 — China motor and ESC export licensing
- Type: fact about published control; confidence high.
- Direct source: https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zc/art/2026/art_b2fa4e60bcd24bdf8c658e476b808785.html
- Date: issued 19 October 2026, effective 18 November 2026; primary page read directly.
- Proves: 9A901.a requires all four motor criteria: rated voltage ≥48 V, rated power ≥3 kW, rated power density ≥3 kW/kg, rated speed ≤3,000 rpm, and the stated drone-propeller design purpose. ESC clause 9A901.b requires its stated linkage to 9A901.a motors and ≥48 V/≥3 kW, without a separate density/speed test. A wedge ESC meeting that definition is itself a licensable type; assess licensing friction on the founder’s own Asia-to-allies route, including any controlled Chinese export leg. Export needs permission; no named destination list in this announcement.
- Does not prove: all 48 V motors are controlled, all small FPV/interceptor systems qualify, or approvals will cease.
- Counterevidence: threshold and purpose conditions limit scope. Decision impact: classify exact SKU; do not sell a blanket embargo story.

## E04 — FCC scope and authorization limits
- Type: fact about regulator FAQ; confidence high for text, not a legal opinion.
- Direct source: https://www.fcc.gov/covered-list-faqs-uas-and-uas-critical-components
- Date: page updated 21 July 2026; Dia Expand All read and saved as fcc-faq-dia.txt.
- Proves: foreign-produced UAS/critical components are addressed; categories include motors, batteries/BMS, flight controllers, communications, sensors/cameras. Nationality of corporate owner does not decide production location. Existing authorizations and use are distinguished from new authorizations. ESCs are not expressly named in the non-exhaustive critical-component list; FAQ limits components to those designed and intended primarily for UAS. Whether the proposed HV ESC is covered or requires authorization is unresolved.
- Does not prove: every motor or battery requires FCC authorization; FAQ expressly says previously exempt devices do not acquire a new authorization requirement. Federal-exclusive systems generally follow separate acquisition rules.
- Counterevidence: this is broader than a China-only restriction but narrower than banning all ownership/import/use of all foreign parts. Decision impact: buyer/channel/SKU eligibility first.
