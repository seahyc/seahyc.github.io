# Demand and affiliate economics: independent source notes

Cutoff/access: **2026-11-03**. This is an evidence slice, not a thesis or approval to run an affiliate agent. Original pages/PDF were opened and read; search snippets were discovery only. Public sources only. Dates below are publisher dates unless explicitly unavailable. No future-dated material is admitted. Undated documentation describes the page observed at cutoff, not a historical policy guarantee.

## Adobe: actual referrals, not autonomous purchases

### Latest available observation

Source: [Adobe, AI traffic now generates 83% higher revenue per visit](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-higher-revenue-per-visit-retail-holiday), published **2026-10-29**.

Verified paraphrase: September 2026 AI-source clickthrough traffic to US retail sites rose **71% YoY**. Its visit-to-purchase conversion rate was **90% higher** than non-AI traffic; revenue per visit **83% higher**. Adobe describes analytics covering more than **1 trillion visits**, **100 million SKUs**, **18 categories**, alongside a separate September survey of more than **5,000 US respondents**.

Denominators: relative conversion compares purchases/visits for AI-source referrals with purchases/visits for non-AI referrals. Revenue/visit compares the same channel cohorts. Traffic growth compares AI referrals against their own year-earlier level. The analytics coverage is not the AI referral sample size; neither exact AI visit counts nor absolute AI share of retail traffic is disclosed here.

Proves: a growing, valuable observed referral channel in Adobe's coverage. Does not prove causal lift, total autonomous checkout share, merchant willingness to pay an independent agent, or publisher commissions. High-intent selection can explain part of the premium. Holiday growth figures elsewhere on this page are forecasts, excluded from observed results.

### Earlier observation: do not present as latest

Source: [Adobe, AI traffic surge and machine readability](https://business.adobe.com/blog/ai-traffic-surge-retail-sites-not-machine-readable), published **2026-04-16**.

Verified paraphrase: March 2026 conversion was **42% above non-AI**, versus **38% below** in March 2025; Q1 2026 AI-source traffic rose **393% YoY**. Companion survey: more than **5,000 US respondents**, **39%** reported having used AI for online shopping; **85% of those users** said it improved their shopping experience. Those are self-reports, distinct from transaction analytics. The later September relative conversion figure supersedes March for a current-channel comparison; the two are different periods, not contradictory estimates of one period. Neither percentage is absolute conversion.

## Worldpay / FIS: willingness and modeled spend

### 2025 original US release

Source: [Worldpay release PDF](https://corporate.worldpay.com/node/7286/pdf), dated **2025-11-05**; [HTML release](https://corporate.worldpay.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ai-agents-set-transform-how-americans-shop-261-billion-predicted). HTML fetch failed; original publisher PDF succeeded.

Verified paraphrase: The Lantern surveyed **8,000 consumers** across US, UK, France, Brazil, China, Singapore and Australia in **August–September 2025**, including **1,000 US consumers**. Respondents first received a description and examples of an agentic shopping assistant. US headline: **44%** willing to let an assistant browse; **6%** willing to buy without checking first. US anticipated purchase share **9%** in five years multiplied by a projected **$2.9 trillion** 2030 US ecommerce market produces **$261 billion**.

Proves: stated willingness under an explained concept; the spending number is a constructed scenario. Does not prove current adoption or realized GMV. Exact question wording, sampling frame, weighting, response rate and uncertainty intervals are not supplied by this release. The younger-group percentage is omitted because subgroup n is unavailable. Do not label this a FIS transaction dataset: its stated commissioning publisher is Worldpay.

### 2026 wave: conditional denominator matters

Source: [Worldpay 2026 report article](https://worldpay.com/en/insights/articles/agentic-commerce-report-2026-out-now), metadata published **2026-09-23**, modified **2026-10-16**; [report landing page](https://worldpay.com/en/agentic-commerce-report); [resource hub](https://worldpay.com/en/agentic-commerce).

Verified paraphrase: second-year Lantern survey covers the same seven named markets and six sectors. Hub/landing advertise **8,000+ consumers**; exact total and country cell counts were not established. Article reports **45% US** and **59% Singapore** answering yes to willingness to shop with an agent. Crucially, **80%** allowing some payment role and **59%** wanting individual purchase approval refer to consumers **already open to AI shopping**, not all consumers. Exact filtered n is undisclosed here.

Proves: reported attitudes, with country variation and retained approval. Does not prove population-wide payment delegation or actual conversion. No percentage should be converted into a count without the filtered n. Full methodology and fieldwork dates remain unresolved: web fetch of report endpoint timed out; direct HTTP returned its promotional landing HTML, not the report. The 2025 44% and 2026 45% US figures are different waves; without full questionnaires/weights do not interpret the small difference as meaningful change.

## Salesforce: broad influenced sales, not autonomous checkout

Source: [2025 holiday shopping data](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/2025-holiday-shopping-data/), published **2026-01-08**, reporting **2025-11-01 through 2025-12-31**.

Verified paraphrase: estimates global online holiday sales **$1.29 trillion**, US **$294 billion**; attributes **$262 billion / 20%** to AI and agents through recommendations and customer engagement. AI-search referrals converted **nine times** as often as social referrals. Method: aggregated activity from more than **1.5 billion global shoppers**, more than **89 countries**, focusing on **18 markets**, then extrapolation to broader retail; proprietary first/third-party inputs and market assumptions.

Proves: Salesforce's modeled broad AI-influenced sales estimate and channel comparison. Does not establish that one-fifth of purchases were autonomously executed. The nine-times comparator is **social**, whereas Adobe compares with **all non-AI channels**; these ratios cannot be combined. Absolute conversion, precise AI-influence assignment formula, causal identification and complete extrapolation weights are absent. The rounded 20% and $262b need not multiply exactly from rounded $1.29t.

## Affiliate economics and agent permissions

### Impact: contract-specific economics; agent partnership described as roadmap

Sources: [Contract History Detail Report](https://help.impact.com/partner/what-would-you-like-to-learn-about/platform-features/reporting-for-partners/contract-history-reports/contract-history-detail-report) (publication date unavailable); [Impact AI page](https://impact.com/ai/) (publication date unavailable).

Verified paraphrase: contract reporting exposes crediting rule, fixed/percentage payout, cookie window, locking period and payment scheduling. AI page explicitly puts onboarding transacting AI agents as a new partner class under **ROADMAP**, while describing infrastructure being built.

Proves: economics and attribution vary by contract; announced direction of travel. Does not establish general AI-agent enrollment availability, permission to automate advertiser sites, payout for zero-click influence, or a network-wide commission rate. An administrative MCP connection is not shopping-agent approval. An attempted separate Impact analytics help page timed out; no claims rely on its search excerpt.

### CJ: rates belong to program terms; tracked is not yet paid

Sources: [Advertiser Lookup API](https://docs.cj.com/docs/advertiserlookup-api) (publication date unavailable); [Publisher Payment Cycle](https://junction.cj.com/article/cookie-dough-understanding-publisher-payment-cycle), **2022-05-05**; [AI Visibility announcement](https://junction.cj.com/article/cj-announces-ai-visibility-and-optimization-solution), **2026-01-12**.

Verified paraphrase: API distinguishes default versus joined advertiser terms and warns that lookup rates omit Situations/Promotional Properties; it directs accurate rate retrieval to Program Terms API. Payment article says new transactions may be corrected or extended, some actions have open-ended locking, and insufficient advertiser funding delays payout. The AI announcement acknowledges pre-click/zero-click measurement blind spots and publishers' difficulty monetizing LLM influence.

Proves: actual signed terms and settled payments govern realizable revenue. Does not establish AI-specific traffic acceptance, a universal rate, or that citation visibility produces a payable event. Historical payment guidance requires rechecking against the actual current contract. No affiliate account or live term was queried.

### Rakuten: valid referral rules constrain an automated flow

Sources: [Affiliate Network Policies](https://rakutenadvertising.com/legal-notices/affiliate-network-policies/) (publication date unavailable); [Mirai launch](https://rakuteninternational.com/news/rakuten-advertising-launches-mirai-affiliate-marketings-first-advanced-ai-optimization-agent), **2026-05-06**.

Verified paraphrase: advertisers decide qualifying actions, amounts and payment conditions. Return Days require an actual valuable referral after user interest; simulated referrals can forfeit commissions. Designated tracking and applicable Return Days govern mobile referrals. Mirai creates/manages advertiser affiliate offers and commission structures.

Proves: advertiser discretion and referral integrity requirements; AI campaign administration exists. Does not prove consumer purchasing agents may receive commission, permission to generate artificial clicks, or universal AI-traffic admission. Mirai is not evidence of shopper-agent authorization. Publisher/program approval, traffic-source disclosure, tracking design, permitted automation, return/cancellation rules and payable order proof remain required for a real pilot.

### Modeling rule (analysis, not sourced revenue)

Do not insert an assumed industry commission range. A defensible model is:

`eligible attributed settled sales × contracted rate − reversals/fees − agent compute − support − acquisition cost`

For fixed CPA replace sales × rate with eligible settled actions × contracted payout. Measure eligibility, attribution survival, returns, settlement lag and operational cost separately. No opened source supplies a transferable realized agent commission or cost per profitable purchase. Unknown policy is **unknown**, not permission and not a proven ban.

## FTC: fake experience, not a blanket prohibition on AI text

Sources: [FTC final-rule announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), **2024-08-14**; [FTC staff Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers), publication date unavailable; accessed at cutoff.

Verified paraphrase: rule effective **2024-10-21** covers fake/false reviews, including invented AI personas or fabricated experience, sentiment-conditioned incentives, and misrepresented independence of controlled review sites. FTC staff distinguishes mere review hosting from creating/buying false reviews and promotional testimonials. Disclosure of influencer/brand relationships is addressed by the FTC Act and Endorsement Guides, not generally this review rule alone.

Proves: specific deceptive-review constraints. Does not prove all AI-assisted genuine reviews unlawful, require every host to investigate every submitted review, or certify an agent-generated product assessment compliant. Operational implication (inference): preserve real provenance, distinguish tested experience from synthesis, and disclose material economic relationships. This source is not a prevalence estimate for fake reviews.
