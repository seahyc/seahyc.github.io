# Pipeline in a Person — research map
Research date: 2026-10-30. Internal dossier; not Hugo content.

## Decision
Should a technical founder build a virtual therapeutic-asset company for biopharma licensees, starting with one extracellular, functionally testable mechanism and outsourced research?

Desired conclusion: pursue a capped evidence-building option only with accountable scientific leadership, a buyer-relevant target-product profile, transferable rights, and stage gates. Reject the claim that a solo outsider can reliably buy a mouse rescue and flip it.

Scope: US-oriented development and buyer diligence; founder may operate elsewhere. 12–24 months to a decision-quality preclinical evidence package, not a clinical-ready or sale guarantee. No experimental protocols, sequences, dosing, or animal-study execution.

## Hypothesis tree (before narrative)
1. Outsourced capacity replaces owned lab: supported by CRO service menus; disconfirmed if the needed assay cannot be transferred reproducibly. Missing: project-specific quotes and queue times. Test: two scoped bids and method-transfer assessment.
2. AI improves candidate generation: supported by prospective design experiments; disconfirmed if target-specific functional yield is poor. Missing: chosen mechanism's prospective yield. Test: compare blinded candidates with benchmark under predeclared functional criteria.
3. Human genetics reduces target uncertainty: supported by retrospective clinical-program association; does not remove therapeutic, delivery, or safety risk. Test: disease expert verifies direction, timing, reversibility, and patient subgroup.
4. Early asset can attract buyer: supported by selected announced deals; contradicted by selection bias and different modalities/platforms/stages. Missing: denominator and buyer appetite. Test: buyer interviews and independent diligence; no sale probability inferred.
5. Small team can finance a useful package: missing transferable all-in prices. Ground-up low/base/high cash allocation scenarios; never presented as quotes. Test: replace assumptions with comparable bids, including failure/repeat costs.

## Strong bear case
The incumbent with human data, patents, manufacturing experience and distribution can beat a novel binder. CROs sell valid tasks even when a program is scientifically wrong. An external operator still owns experiment interpretation and repeated failures. A clean mouse effect may be species-specific, short-lived, unmanufacturable, unprotectable, or commercially irrelevant. One person's operational bandwidth and scientific blind spots can be binding.

## Wedge and falsifier
Hypothesis: one extracellular inhibitory mechanism, supported in humans, with a quantitative functional assay, a clinically meaningful differentiation goal, and a tractable path to human-relevant exposure. Founder owns an auditable design–test–decision loop with a retained disease/translational lead. Fail if no defensible improvement, no transferable assay, no rights, or bids cannot fund next gate plus reserve. Named approved diseases in narrative are benchmarks, not recommended targets.

## Next action
Owner: founder + retained translational scientist. First 30 days: compare three target-product profiles, get two assay feasibility bids for finalist, independent IP screen and buyer feedback. Stop before synthesis if no scientist signs the mechanistic rationale or no measurable comparator-relative advantage exists.
