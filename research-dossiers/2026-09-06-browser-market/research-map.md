# Research map: browser-automation market

Dossier date 2026-09-06 (source research). Fieldbook build and claim re-verification: 2026-11-21. Source folder copied unmodified into `source-research/`.

## Founder decision

Should a founder build a company for operators who run recurring multi-step browser work (customer-authorised portals), starting with one vertical portal-connection workflow, on the strength of a stateful browser / anti-bot market signal?

Desired reader conclusion: the triggering post does not validate a generic stateful anti-bot API. A narrow, paid, one-workflow wedge is worth a 90-day test, with stop gates set in advance.

Core assumptions (all unverified): operators exist with recurring portal work that costs real money; existing browser infrastructure (Browserbase, Steel, Bright Data, Skyvern) leaves a business-outcome gap; that gap survives a comparison on the buyer's own workload.

What would disprove it: an existing vendor solves the buyer's problem by ordinary configuration; buyers will not pay for accepted outcomes; each customer needs unrelated custom engineering; contribution margin after human exception work stays below 50%.

## Competing theses

1. Generic stateful anti-bot browser API (the proposed idea). Counter: Browserbase, Steel, Bright Data already advertise persistent state, bot-detection handling and replay.
2. Managed scraping is a durable purchase even for builders who can code (Levels retained ScrapingBee; Klaas cites edge cases). Counter: the Apify survey sample is mostly in-house builders, and the post shows one customer.
3. Vertical portal-connection runtime (the wedge). Counter: bespoke service work; RPA and Skyvern alternatives.
4. Communications runtime (ranked 1 in the source infrastructure thesis). Outside this fieldbook's evidence; the source says current code and live marketplace data were not audited.

## Measurable variables

Cost per accepted business result; operator minutes per exception; contribution margin after human work; bespoke-engineering hours per added customer; paid-pilot conversion; renewal after 60 days.

## Evidence thinness (stated up front)

Primary demand evidence is one founder's post and four replies. Competitor evidence is vendor marketing pages. No buyer interviews, billing data or failure logs exist. Revenue and market size for the wedge are unknown.
