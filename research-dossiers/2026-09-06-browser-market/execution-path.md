# Execution path: from one portal workflow to a browser-runtime company

Every budget, calendar and numeric gate is a founder-selected planning assumption set in advance so it can fail. None is derived from the sources. Sources establish precedents (ledger IDs in brackets), not demand for this wedge. No outreach, quote, hire or pilot has occurred.

## Stage sequence

| Stage | Window | What the founder builds | What it earns or proves | Funds / de-risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Find the job | days 1-30 | Interview list, baseline sheet, no product | Which recurring portal workflow costs a buyer real money and still fails | Chooses one vertical |
| 1 Paid pilots | days 31-90 | Hand-supervised service for 3 customers on the same outcome, built on an existing browser vendor | Prepaid pilot fees; measured cost per accepted result | Pays the next 3 months |
| 2 Repeatable workflow | months 4-12 | Productised connector for the same workflow, exception queue, evidence log | 10 paying customers; margin after human work | Hires first ops person |
| 3 Runtime | months 12-24 | Second and third workflow on the same state layer; self-serve API only if reuse is proven | Whether this is infrastructure or a services business | Decides venture vs bootstrap |

Gates (proposed): Stage 0 to 1: at least 3 buyers with the same workflow, a recent failure log, and monthly cost (labor plus tools) of at least $1,000. Stage 1 to 2: 3 prepaid pilots; at least 30% fewer failed or manual items than the buyer's baseline on their own workload; contribution margin at least 50% after exception labor; at most 20% of engineering hours on customer-specific work. Stage 2 to 3: 10 paying customers, 60-day renewal at least 80%, new customer onboarded in at most 8 engineer-hours, second workflow added in under 4 weeks.

## Buyers and demand

Named buyer types to test (hypotheses): operations or finance teams that pull invoices, orders or statuses from several supplier portals; agencies that run portal work for clients. Routes: warm intros from the founder's network, the public discussions where this research started, vendor communities of Browserbase and Steel, the Apify Store as a later channel (80% creator share under pay-per-event, [E22]). Commitment to ask for: a prepaid pilot, read access to the last 30 days of failures, and a named reviewer who accepts or rejects outputs. A verbal "interesting" does not pass.

## Credibility

Publish a benchmark on customer-approved workloads: accepted results, cost per accepted result, human minutes, and a failure taxonomy, versus the buyer's existing provider. Use design-partner references. Where portal owners cooperate, adopt signed-agent identity ([E07]), noting it helps only where a site can read and validate it (inference; E07 gives no count of such sites). Advisors: one operator from the chosen vertical; one engineer who has run browser fleets. Avoid unverifiable throughput claims, the pattern the research found in [E04].

## Knowledge and team

The founder must learn the vertical's documents and exception types, procurement approval, and portal contract terms. Hires in order: an exceptions operator (human work sets the margin), a vertical advisor, then an engineer for connector maintenance. Co-founder profile: vertical operator or seller who has run that workflow. Where to find them: the interviewed buyers themselves. This is an assumption, not researched.

## Capital path

| Stage | Assumed cash need | Sources in order |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | $5,000-12,000 | Founder savings, then pilot fees |
| 2 | $100,000-300,000 | Revenue, small angel round or accelerator |
| 3 | $1,000,000-5,000,000 | Venture only if reuse across workflows is proven; otherwise revenue |

Precedents: ScrapingBee was described as mostly bootstrapped to $5M ARR with four people, then sold in an eight-figure all-cash deal [E11, E12]. Browserbase is reported to have raised about $67.5M by a $40M Series B at $300M [E19, secondary]. These are two different shapes; neither shows that a new entrant can repeat them. Nationality or ownership constraints on funding were not researched.

## Legal path

Public pages and gated systems are treated differently in the US (hiQ v. LinkedIn, [E20]); Singapore's Computer Misuse Act covers unauthorised access [E21]. Contract, copyright and tort claims sit outside both. The route: only customer-authorised accounts with the customer's own credentials, a written authorisation per portal, counsel review of each portal's terms, and no product claim of bypassing a protection. Personal-data rules were not researched.

## Personal outcome

Stage 0-1: little or no salary; pilot fees cover costs. Stage 2: salary from contribution once margin passes the gate. Stage 3: dividends if bootstrapped; equity value only if a venture path is taken. The only exit precedent is an eight-figure all-cash sale [E11]; the founders' share and terms are unknown. No payout number is projected.

## Falsifiers

- Existing vendor configuration fixes the buyer's failures (Stage 0 stop).
- Buyers want a one-off script handoff, not a monthly service.
- Cost per accepted result is not at least 30% lower than the buyer's baseline.
- Contribution margin below 50% after exception labor, or over 20% of engineering time spent per customer.
- Fewer than 3 prepaid pilots by day 90 (stop or narrow the vertical once; two failed verticals end the thesis).
